    General Information¶

    Document Objective¶

    This Physical Security Standard provides detailed requirements for physical security controls at Community Fibre to prevent unauthorised access, damage, or interference with information assets and infrastructure. It supports the ISMS by detailing controls from ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annexe A.7, and the NCSC Cyber Assessment Framework Principle B2 aligns with regulatory requirements, including the Telecommunications (Security) Act 2021.

    This document meets the following clauses:

    • Information Security Management System ISO/IEC 27001:2022 -- A.7: Physical controls

    • NCSC Cyber Assessment Framework (CAF) v3.1 - Principle B2

    • The Electronic Communications (Security Measures) Regulations 2022 Reg 3(3)(a) Protect network from external threats (including physical); Code of Practice sections on site protection, access, monitoring, and environmental resilience

    Policy¶

    Scope and principles¶

    This standard applies to all in-scope physical locations and assets, personnel, and contractors at Community Fibre, including offices, data centres, and exchanges. It covers the lifecycle of physical assets and integrates with logical security measures.

    Key principles, informed by ISO 27001 and NCSC Cyber Assessment Framework:

    • Establish perimeters and monitoring to prevent and detect unauthorised access.

    • Controls proportionate to asset sensitivity (per ISP037) and risks (e.g., exposed edge equipment).

    • Protect against environmental threats and ensure continuity.

    • Physical security supports logical controls (e.g., no physical access bypassing authentication).

    Physical security is managed by Network Technology, Network Operations, Security Operations, and IT, with oversight from the ISMS Board.

    Physical security perimeter (A.7.1)¶

    # Purpose¶

    To prevent unauthorised physical access, damage and interference to the organisation's information and other associated assets. [1]

    • Areas to consider for physical security include:

    • Head office

    • Data centres

    • Exchanges

    • Remote working locations

    • Define security perimeters, including their locations and strengths, based on security requirements for the assets inside the area.

    • For sites where network equipment is located, physically and logically separate vulnerable equipment (exposed edge, e.g., CPE, OLT, roadside cabinets) from security-critical functions; no sensitive data stored in exposed edges.

    • Any sensitive equipment that is within the public domain will have all CFL branding removed from the public view, and instead use unique identifiers

    • Ensure physically secure perimeters for buildings or sites with sensitive processing facilities, eliminating gaps or easy break-in areas. Employ robust construction materials for exterior roofs, walls, ceilings, and floors.

    • Secure external doors from unauthorised access using mechanisms such as bars, alarms, and locks.

    • Ensure that doors and windows are securely locked when unattended. Additionally, consider implementing external protective measures for windows, particularly those on the ground floor, as well as for ventilation points.

    • Ensure that fire doors are equipped with alarms, monitored, and routinely tested within security perimeters, including walls, to meet the required resistance standards. Additionally, ensure that failsafe operation protocols are strictly adhered to.

    • Conduct perimeter inspections and testing at least annually to confirm integrity.

    • Conduct monthly spot-checks.

    Physical entry controls (A.7.2)¶

    # Purpose¶

    To ensure that only authorised physical access to the organisation's information and other associated assets occurs. [1]

    # General¶

    For the areas where we must consider physical security, refer to A.7.1: Physical security perimeter.

    Our main office is within a serviced office facility. Therefore, many of the requirements for Physical entry controls are the responsibility of the landlord as one of our service suppliers. Our assurance for physical entry is managed through the controls relating to Supplier relationships: A.5.19, A.5.20, A.5.21 and A.5.22

    • Unless monitored by reception staff during regular working hours, initial building access is controlled with a building access code, which is known only to the tenants in the building, building security and facilities management staff.

    • Key parts of the building, including where our offices are based, are only accessible via perimeter pass or individual identifiable code provided to members of staff to be able to access appropriate areas of the building for the specified role, responsibility and requirement to conduct their role

    • All cleaners and security guards around the building are vetted and managed by the supplier. Cleaning within our office is carried out by building service staff.

    # Visitors¶

    A reception area, constituting part of the serviced office facility, is operational during standard working hours and serves to receive guests and oversee their entry and exit.

    • Visitor logs are held by reception staff.

    • Staff issued with perimeter passes must always carry them whilst in the building.

    • All visitors are supervised within our offices.

    # Delivery and loading areas and incoming material¶

    The delivery and loading area is under the responsibility of the supplier of the serviced office facility. However, upon assessment of this area, we have observed the following:

    The delivery and loading area is located at the rear of the building and is regulated as follows:

    • The internal and external doors must be secured and kept locked at all times when not in use.

    • When the external doors are open, the internal doors must be kept securely locked, and vice versa.

    • CCTV cameras are installed both externally and internally.

    # Sensitive processing areas¶

    Data centres and exchange facilities are sites that house security-critical infrastructure and possess distinct threat profiles associated with increased risks compared to other areas, such as our headquarters. Access to these locations, therefore:

    • Access is restricted solely to authorised personnel. There exists a procedure for the provisioning, review, updating, and revocation of access rights.

    • Securely maintain a comprehensive audit trail of all access and authentication information.

    • Have a process and technical mechanisms for access management. (e.g., key exchange, key audit)

    • Authentication can include access cards, biometrics or multi-factor authentication, such as an access card and PIN.

    • Physical security must be overseen by on-site security personnel.

    • Personal belongings must be inspected upon entry and exit.

    • Require visible identification to be worn; unaccompanied visitors with no visible identification must be reported to security personnel.

    • Supplier personnel must only be granted temporary access, which must be authorised and monitored.

    • Lower risk tolerance should be maintained in multi-tenanted sites.

    • A key management process must be maintained to manage physical keys or codes. An annual key audit must be completed.

    Securing offices, rooms, and facilities (A.7.3)¶

    # Purpose¶

    To prevent unauthorised physical access, damage and interference to the organisation's information and other associated assets in offices, rooms and facilities. [1]

    # Sensitive processing areas¶

    • Shared sites such as data centres and exchanges must employ consistent security measures and maintain segregation from co-tenants.

    • Must not be easily identifiable by unauthorised persons.

    • Critical facilities should be located away from public access.

    • Buildings should be non-descript; directory listing must not disclose sensitive processing or security-critical areas.

    • Alarm codes are to be changed immediately, and PIN codes invalidated when employees depart from the organisation, in accordance with the exit and change procedures outlined in section A.5.18: Access rights.

    • We adhere to standard health and safety protocols as recommended by the facilities providers (offices or data centres).

    # Office¶

    • Perimeter passes must only allow tenants into the relevant and authorised parts of the building.

    • Shared private meeting facilities are available for use by staff and visitors. The supplier manages these facilities, and all staff members are responsible for ensuring that any personal or organisational assets, as well as other information, are removed before vacating the room.

    • If a shared meeting room is used over multiple days, then, if appropriate, materials can be left overnight so long as;

    • The room is locked and secured

    • It is agreed with the facilities supplier that no cleaning or other room changes take place (if sensitive materials are in place); and

    • the material is obscured from view by other tenants, e.g. placing blinds over the windows looking into the hallway.

    Physical security monitoring (A.7.4)¶

    # Purpose¶

    To detect and deter unauthorised physical access. [1]

    # Sensitive processing areas¶
    • Physical premises should be monitored by surveillance systems, which can include guards, alarms, CCTV and physical security information management software either managed internally or by a monitoring service provider.

    • Access to buildings that house critical systems should be continuously monitored to detect unauthorised access or suspicious behaviour (e.g., CCTV, intrusion detection systems) in sensitive processing areas, with logs or records retained for at least 13 months.

    • Motion sensors and contact alarm triggers must be installed to applicable standards and periodically tested.

    • Alarms should cover all external doors and accessible windows. Unoccupied areas should always be alarmed.

    # Office¶

    • Our principal office is located within a serviced office facility; consequently, the landlord assumes responsibility for many of the requirements pertaining to physical security monitoring.

    • Our assurance for Physical security monitoring is managed through the controls relating to Supplier relationships: A.5.19, A.5.20, A.5.21 and A.5.22.

    Protecting against physical and environmental threats (A.7.5)¶

    # Purpose¶

    To prevent or reduce the consequences of events originating from physical and environmental threats. [1]

    # Sensitive processing areas¶

    • Risk assessments must be conducted to identify the potential consequences of physical and environmental threats before beginning critical operations.

    • Consider risks arising from physical and environmental threats, including fire, flooding, earthquakes, explosions, civil unrest, toxic waste, emissions, and other natural or human-made disasters. Seek specialised consultation on effective management strategies for these concerns.

    • Locate and construct physical premises considering local topography (e.g., elevation, bodies of water, fault lines) and urban threats (e.g., high-profile areas for unrest, crime, or terrorism).

    • Fire suppression systems must be able to detect and send alarms or contain fire damage at an early stage.

    • Flood detection systems must be able to detect and send alarms or contain flood damage at an early stage and not damage essential IT infrastructure or key systems.

    • Suppression systems should not harm essential CFL IT and Infrastructure systems.

    • Regular maintenance/servicing must be conducted within the recommended manufacturers \' guidelines

    • Systems must be installed to protect both server and client information systems against electrical surges or similar events to minimise the consequences of such events.

    # Office¶
    • The provider of serviced offices bears the obligation to safeguard the buildings from external and environmental hazards. This encompasses the installation of fire extinguishers and related control measures in accordance with established standards. Our assurance of protection against these risks is governed by the controls related to Supplier relationships: A.5.19, A.5.20, A.5.21, and A.5.22

    Working in secure areas (A.7.6)¶

    # Purpose¶

    To protect information and other associated assets in secure areas from damage and unauthorised interference by personnel working in these areas. [1]

    # Sensitive processing areas¶

    • Knowledge of activities and locations for areas containing security-critical infrastructure is disclosed on a need-to-know basis.

    • No work by third parties or contractors in areas containing security-critical infrastructure without supervision.

    • Photographs and video recordings in areas containing security-critical infrastructure may only be taken if authorised by Network Operations or Technology management.

    • Only approved end-user devices are permitted to enter and operate in areas containing security-critical infrastructure.

    # Office¶

    • Our Head Office environment holds no security-critical services, and our assets are hosted within secure datacentre and in specific cloud locations; no area has been determined as a sensitive processing area.

    • Breakout or meeting rooms must be used in planning or other sensitive customer work, engaging people on a 'need-to-know' basis to avoid risk of information leakage into the main open-plan office, where it is not required to be shared.

    • Staff who work on security-critical services will do so using privileged access workstations in an area of the office that prevents other staff from seeing their work or will perform this work from home (e.g. Network engineers).

    • Currently, there is no need to display emergency procedures above and beyond the standard fire and emergency exit notices provided by the landlord of the serviced office building where our office is located.

    • Staff and others using our offices are reminded of the need to avoid leaving "Confidential" or "Strictly Confidential" materials where others might see them (e.g. through use of simple messages by the room exit and staff training). Secure working is further reinforced via our policy for Remote Working.

    Clear desk and clear screen (A.7.7)¶

    # Purpose¶

    To reduce the risks of unauthorised access, loss, and damage to information on desks, screens and in other accessible locations during and outside regular working hours. [1]

    • "Strictly confidential" information must not be left alone or exposed to unauthorised users at any point.

    • "Confidential" information on paper or electronic storage media, must be secured when not needed, especially when the office is vacated (ideally in paperless form in the first instance, otherwise locked in the desk pedestal, filing cabinet or other furniture at home or in the office)

    • Incoming and outgoing mail points are monitored by facilities management staff.

    • Documents containing "Confidential", or "Strictly confidential" information must be removed from printers immediately.

    • Devices must have a lock screen enabled.

    • There is no need to physically secure devices if the room in which the device is held has adequate security (e.g., door locks, or it is within trusted premises)

    • If there is any concern for safety, then employees must take their device with them.

    • Care must be taken to protect Confidential or Strictly Confidential when in public areas as data may be disclosed inadvertently or intentionally. Be careful where you take a call that covers sensitive information and try to position yourself in a busy area so that your device screen cannot be seen, or you cannot be overheard..

    Equipment siting and protection (A.7.8)¶

    # Purpose¶

    To mitigate risks associated with physical and environmental threats, as well as unauthorised access and damage.

    • Site equipment to minimise unnecessary access to work areas and prevent unauthorised entry. (e.g., away from windows, elevated for flood protection).

    • Position sensitive data-processing facilities to minimise the risk of unauthorised viewing during operation.

    • Implement controls to reduce physical and environmental risks (e.g., theft, fire, explosives, smoke, water/supply failure, dust, vibration, chemicals, electrical/communications interference, electromagnetic radiation, vandalism).

    • Clear, established rules for eating, drinking, and smoking near information processing facilities.

    • Monitor environmental conditions such as temperature and humidity that could impact facility operations.

    • Install lightning protection on all buildings and install filters on incoming power and communication lines.

    • Use specialised protections (e.g., keyboard membranes) for equipment in industrial settings.

    • Protect equipment containing confidential information from risks of electromagnetic leakage.

    • Physically segregate organisation-managed facilities from external ones.

    Security of assets off-premises (A.7.9)¶

    # Purpose¶

    To prevent loss, damage, theft or compromise of off-site devices and interruption to the organisation's operations. [1]

    • Avoid leaving equipment and storage media unattended in public or unsecured places.

    • Manage storage media lifecycle: Inventory, secure transport, and encryption for sensitive data.

    • Prohibit unauthorised removal; use approved media for transfers.

    • Follow manufacturers\' instructions for protection (e.g., against electromagnetic fields, water, heat, humidity, dust).

    • When transferring off-premises equipment between individuals or parties, keep a chain-of-custody log with responsible names and organisations. Securely delete unnecessary information before the transfer.

    • Require authorisation before removing equipment and media from premises; record all removals for audit trails (see 5.14).

    • Protect against unauthorised viewing (for example, on public transport) and shoulder surfing.

    • Activate location tracking and remote data wiping.

    For permanently installed equipment outside premises (e.g., Exchanges, roadside cabinets), which face higher risks of damage, theft, or eavesdropping, consider location-specific measures including:

    • Physical security monitoring (see 7.4)

    • Protection against physical and environmental threats (see 7.5)

    • Physical access and tamper-proofing controls (e.g., secure boot)

    • Logical access controls.

    Storage media (A.7.10)¶

    # Purpose¶

    To ensure that only authorised disclosure, modification, removal, or destruction of information on storage media is permitted. [1]

    Consider these guidelines for managing removable storage media.

    • Establish and communicate a policy specific to removable storage media to users and handlers.

    • Require authorisation before removing storage media from the organisation and record removals for audit purposes.

    • Store media in a safe, secure environment according to information classification, protecting against environmental threats (e.g., heat, moisture, humidity, electronic fields, ageing) following manufacturers\' specifications.

    • Use cryptographic techniques to protect sensitive or integrity-critical data on removable media.

    • Transfer information to new media before degradation renders it unreadable.

    • Store multiple copies of valuable information on separate media to minimise the risk of damage or loss.

    • Register removable media to minimise information loss risks.

    • Enable removable media ports (e.g., SD card slots, USB ports) only if organisationally necessary.

    • Monitor information transfers to removable media when used.

    • Information risks unauthorised access, misuse, or corruption during physical transport (e.g., via post or courier).

    Note: Media includes paper documents. Apply security measures from 5.14 for physical transfers.

    Refer to ISP044-Secure Disposal Policy for secure disposal and destruction of media containing data.

    Supporting utilities (A.7.11)¶

    # Purpose¶

    To prevent loss, damage or compromise of information and other associated assets, or interruption to the organisation's operations due to failure and disruption of supporting utilities. [1]

    • Configure, operate, and maintain utility-supporting equipment in accordance with the manufacturers\' specifications.

    • Regularly evaluate utilities to ensure they can support business growth and coordinate effectively with other utilities.

    • Regularly check and test utility-supporting equipment to ensure it functions correctly.

    • If necessary, implement alarms to detect utility malfunctions.

    • If required, supply utilities with multiple feeds and varied physical routing.

    • If connected to a network, keep utility-supporting equipment on a separate network from information processing facilities.

    • Connect utility-supporting equipment to the internet only when necessary and secure it.

    • Provide emergency lighting and communication systems. Locate emergency switches and valves for power, water, gas, or other utilities near emergency exits or equipment rooms.

    • Record and provide emergency contact details for outages.

    Other information: Enhance network connectivity redundancy by establishing multiple routes from various utility providers.

    Cabling security (A.7.12)¶

    # Purpose¶

    To prevent loss, damage, theft, or compromise of information and other associated assets, and to prevent interruptions to the organisation's operations related to power and communications cabling. [1]

    • Install power and telecommunications lines underground within information processing facilities where feasible, or use suitable alternative protections (e.g., floor cable protectors, utility poles). For underground cables, safeguard against accidental cuts (e.g., armoured conduits, presence signals).

    • Keep power cables separate from communications cables to avoid interference.

    • For sensitive or critical systems, consider implementing supplementary controls:

    • Install armoured conduits, locked rooms or boxes, and alarms at inspection or termination points.

    • Use electromagnetic shielding for cables.

    • Perform regular technical checks and physical inspections to identify unauthorised attached devices.

    • Manage access to patch panels and cable rooms (e.g., mechanical keys, PINs).

    • Utilise fibre-optic cables.

    • Clear and recognisable cable labels at each end with source and destination details for physical identification and inspection.

    • Consult a specialist for advice on managing risks from cabling incidents or malfunctions.

    • Conduct tampering checks when visiting areas that are accessible to the public, and random checks on other locations annually. Report and record any s

    Other information: Power and telecommunications cabling may be shared among multiple organisations in co-located premises.

    Equipment maintenance (A.7.13)¶

    # Purpose¶

    To prevent loss, damage, theft or compromise of information and other associated assets and interruption to the organisation's operations caused by lack of maintenance. [1]

    • Maintain equipment according to the supplier\'s recommended service intervals and specifications.

    • Establish and oversee an organisational maintenance programme.

    • Allow only authorised personnel to carry out repairs and maintenance.

    • Record all suspected or actual faults and preventive or corrective maintenance.

    • Implement suitable controls for scheduled maintenance, taking into account whether on-site or external personnel are involved; ensure maintenance staff sign confidentiality agreements.

    • Supervise on-site maintenance personnel.

    • Authorise and oversee remote maintenance access.

    • Implement off-premises asset security measures (see 7.9) if information-containing equipment is taken for maintenance.

    • Adhere to insurance-mandated maintenance obligations.

    • Inspect equipment post-maintenance for tampering and proper function before reactivation.

    • Use secure disposal or reuse measures (see 7.14) when equipment is to be disposed of.

    Other information: Equipment encompasses information processing facility components, UPS and batteries, power generators, alternators, converters, and physical intrusion systems.

    Secure disposal or re-use of equipment (A.7.14)¶

    # Purpose¶

    To prevent leakage of information from equipment to be disposed of or reused. [1]

    • Check equipment for stored media before disposal or reuse.

    • Physically destroy storage media containing confidential or copyrighted information or delete/overwrite data using irreversible methods, not standard deletion. Refer to 7.10 for secure disposal and contact Security Operations for guidance on deletion.

    • Remove labels or markings that identify organisation, classification, owner, system, or network before disposal, including resale or donation.

    • Consider removing security controls (e.g., access controls, surveillance equipment) at lease end or move-out, depending on:

    • Lease requirement to restore to original condition

    • Risks of leaving sensitive data (e.g., user lists, videos/images) for the next tenant

    • Reuse potential at new facility.

    Other information: For damaged equipment with storage media, perform a risk assessment to determine physical destruction vs. repair/discard. Careless disposal/reuse can compromise information.

    Please refer to the ISP044-Secure Disposal Policy.pdf for guidance.

    Regulatory requirements¶

    • Physical security measures protect against external threats (Reg 3), including site perimeters, access restrictions, and monitoring.

    • For shared/third-party sites: Ensure equivalent security (CoP Chap 6).

    • Testing: Include physical access simulations in penetration tests (CoP Chap 13).

    • Resilience: Address environmental/geopolitical threats in continuity planning (CoP Para 2.95).

    Monitoring and review¶

    • Conduct annual audits and risk assessments, review after incidents or changes.

    • Integrate physical security incidents into ISMS reporting.

    Responsibilities¶

    • Network Operations and Network Technology: Implement and maintain controls.

    • Security Operations: Monitor and respond to incidents.

    • All Staff: Report issues; comply with access rules.

    • ISMS Board: Oversight and approval.
