    Introduction¶

    We recognise the transformative potential of AI tools but also acknowledge the risks they pose to data privacy and security. This policy aims to guide employees in using AI responsibly, ensuring compliance with current regulations including GDPR and UK data protection laws. It specifically addresses the responsible handling of sensitive data and proprietary company and customer information. As AI becomes more prevalent in day-to-day work, this policy ensures alignment with ethics, laws, and core values.

    Scope¶

    This policy applies to all employees, contractors, and third parties who interact with AI tools within Community Fibre. This includes Community Fibre provided and publicly available AI tools as well as any company data used in these tools. Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools have the potential to automate tasks, improve decision-making, and provide valuable insights. However, their use also presents challenges related to information security and data protection. This policy guides employees on safe and secure AI tool usage, especially when handling potentially sensitive information.

    Principles and Values¶

    Introduction¶

    AI Use Principles and Values apply to all Employees on the use or implementation of AI within Community Fibre, including the use of Large Language Models (LLM), Generic AI, Generative AI, etc. The design principles from chapter "4. AI System Onboarding Requirements" also applies when designing or implementing an AI system.

    Roles & Responsibilities¶

    A detailed overview of specific actions, along with the designated owners for each action, is available in Section 5.7, titled \"Overview of All Actions with Owners.\"

    Principles and Values¶

    Be open and transparent about the use of AI¶

    Employees must be open and transparent about the use of AI tools within their departments and teams. This includes sharing the purpose of the tool, how it is being utilised, and the potential impact on processes or decision-making.

    Do not use personal data or confidential information¶

    It is strictly prohibited to use personal data as defined in the Community Fibre Data Protection Policy, such as telephone numbers, call detail records, email-addresses or financial information, for AI applications without a proper legal ground and security & data protection measures. Confidential business data with a competitive advantage must also be carefully protected.

    An AI Risk Assessment must be performed in case you need to process personal data and/or confidential information with an AI tool.

    Use AI within your Expertise¶

    Use AI tools only within your own domain of knowledge or expertise. This means you should understand the output of the AI and be able to verify it.

    Guard Against Bias and Discrimination¶

    You must be aware of potential bias in AI systems, especially when these systems make decisions regarding customer interactions. You should take care to avoid producing discriminatory results.

    Trust, but Verify¶

    Never solely rely on the outcome of AI tools. Always check the results of AI tools to ensure they remain accurate and relevant.

    Hold yourself accountable for AI use¶

    You are personally responsible for the proper use of AI tools and must report and rectify any errors or issues. You are responsible for the outcome as it was produced by yourself.

    Consider implications of AI use¶

    Always consider whether the use of AI tools is responsible, especially when it affects customers.

    Be Aware of the AI Tool and its Related Risks¶

    As a user of AI tools, you must be fully aware of the capabilities, limitations, and potential risks associated with the use of these tools. This principle emphasises the importance of understanding AI systems to make informed and responsible decisions.

    Mandatory AI Risk Assessment¶

    If you intend to integrate an AI system into Community Fibre network or process confidential information (see ISP037-Information Classification and Handling Policy) or personal data within an AI system, a prior AI Risk Assessment is mandatory. This assessment is essential to proactively address compliance, privacy and security risks associated with the AI system\'s deployment and data handling practices.

    Report issues or concerns¶

    If you notice or experience any issues regarding AI system performance or ethical implications, report this immediately to dpo@communityfibre.co.uk.

    Progressive Implementation of AI Tools¶

    When AI functionalities are progressively integrated into existing software (e.g., Office 365, Adobe), these also need to be assessed. The integration should pass through an AI Risk Assessment process to ensure compliance with Community Fibre policies, even if the software is Community Fibre IT-provided. This ensures that any new AI capabilities, such as Microsoft Copilot, do not inadvertently expose personal or confidential information.

    Design & Implementation of AI tools: Requirements¶

    Introduction¶

    This chapter outlines the critical design requirements for ensuring the trustworthiness of AI systems within Community Fibre. It underscores the need to address a multitude of criteria valued by stakeholders. It highlights key characteristics, including validity, reliability, safety, security, accountability, transparency, explainability, interpretability, privacy enhancement and fairness with bias management.

    These requirements must be carefully balanced within the specific context of AI system deployment to mitigate negative consequences effectively. [ref. 21]

    Roles & Responsibilities¶

    A detailed overview of specific actions, along with the designated owners for each action, is available in Section 5.7, titled \"Overview of All Actions with Owners.\"

    Design Requirements¶

    Valid and Reliable¶

    AI systems must be validated for their specific intended use, ensuring accuracy and reliability. Ongoing testing and monitoring are required to confirm systems perform as intended.

    Safe¶

    AI systems should be designed, developed, and deployed to avoid endangering human life, health, property, or the environment.

    Secure and Resilient¶

    AI systems must be secure against unauthorised access and use, maintaining confidentiality, integrity, and availability. Refer to Community Fibre ISP001-ISMS Mandatory Clauses for applicable security policies, standards and guidelines.

    Systems should be resilient, able to withstand and recover from adverse events and changes in their environment.

    Accountable and Transparent¶

    AI systems must have clear accountability mechanisms, e.g. ensuring that operations, decisions, and methodologies of AI systems are understandable and traceable.

    Transparency in AI operations, including decision-making processes, should be maintained, and documented.

    Explainable and Interpretable¶

    AI systems should be explainable, with explicit representation of their operational mechanisms. Generic AI might have some limitations in terms of providing assurance of privacy and security such as general accuracy, lack of depth, lack of QA, resource intensive, lack of explainability (model design and operation may be difficult to explain).

    When the use of an AI system has a legally binding effect on a data subject, or similarly significantly affects them, they must be given meaningful information about the logic of the AI system involved. Additional requirements from the Community Fibre Data Protection Policy apply.

    Privacy-Enhanced (if personal data will be processed)¶

    AI systems should adhere to the Community Fibre Data Protection Policy protecting user autonomy and data. A data privacy impact assessment should be performed to guide AI system design to enhance privacy, including using privacy-enhancing technologies (. e.g., anonymisation and encryption).

    Fair -- with Harmful Bias Managed¶

    AI systems must be designed and operated to manage and mitigate harmful biases.

    The AI system treats everyone equally, no matter their background or what group they belong to. This means the system should not make decisions that unfairly favour one group over another or discriminate based on things like a person\'s race, gender, age, or any other personal characteristic.

    Compliant Design¶

    AI Systems must be frequently reviewed using the assessments referred to in 4.4, to ensure it stays compliant with applicable laws and regulations, e.g. the EU AI Act and the DPA 2018/EU GDPR.

    Accountability and Governance¶

    Introduction¶

    When introducing a new AI system at Community Fibre, it\'s essential to fully understand its role, the risks involved, and its purpose. To ensure smooth integration, Community Fibre has established specific onboarding requirements. These requirements are designed to identify and address the risks and actions associated with each AI tool based on its unique features and intended use. This approach helps us integrate AI systems effectively and responsibly, ensuring they align with our organisational goals and standards.

    Roles & Responsibilities¶

    A detailed overview of specific actions, along with the designated owners for each action, is available in Section 5.7, titled \"Overview of All Actions with Owners.\"

    AI tools in existing systems¶

    When AI functionalities are progressively integrated into existing software (e.g., Office 365, Adobe), these also need to be assessed. The integration should pass through an AI Risk Assessment process to ensure compliance with Community Fibre policies, even if the software is Community Fibre IT-provided. This ensures that any new AI capabilities, such as Microsoft Copilot, do not inadvertently expose personal or confidential information.

    AI systems for one-off or testing purposes¶

    When using AI tools [ref. 13, 15, 16] for singular tasks or testing purposes the data shall remain within a secure and controlled environment; this shall be enforced contractually and with technical controls that can be demonstrated, and the AI System Registration and Intake Process shall be completed as part of project initiation or before one-off use.

    Integrated AI Functionalities¶

    New AI features in existing software shall be evaluated for potential risks, especially regarding data privacy and security. Employees must be trained on the use of AI functionalities and made aware of risks to privacy and security.

    AI System Registration and Intake Process¶

    Every integrated AI system and tool proposed or in use within Community Fibre shall be registered using the designated AI Intake Form. The purpose of the Intake Form is:

    • To collect essential information about the AI system, including its purpose, data usage, and technical specifications.

    • To conduct an initial evaluation of potential risks associated with the AI system, it can be decided, based on the initial assessment, that the AI system poses too high a risk for use in Community Fibre.

    • To decide whether a comprehensive Privacy Assessment and/or AI Risk Assessment is necessary based on the preliminary findings.

    The intake for can be found here: AI System Intake - CFL IT Support Portal - Jira Service Management (atlassian.net)

    The information gathered from the AI Intake Form will be used to maintain an up-to-date AI Register, which serves as a centralised record of all AI systems and tools used within Community Fibre.

    AI Register Maintenance¶

    Governance, Risk & Compliance will be responsible for maintaining the AI Register. This role includes:

    • Keeping oversight to ensure that the AI Register accurately reflects all AI systems and tools in use and that they are compliant with Community Fibre's AI policies and relevant regulations.

    • Supporting AI project teams, IT, legal, and compliance departments to ensure complete and accurate information is captured in the AI Register.

    • Managing access to the AI Register, ensuring that relevant stakeholders have the necessary visibility while maintaining confidentiality and data security.

    • Technical Owners are responsible for initiating the AI intake process, providing essential information about the AI system, and ensuring the information remains current.

    Thresholds for Conducting Assessments¶

    An AI Risk Assessment should be conducted:¶

    For any new AI system or major update to an existing system covering one or more below:

    • If the AI system has the potential to significantly impact individuals, business operations, or decision-making processes.

    • When the AI system processes or potentially processes confidential information or strictly confidential data. (ISP037-Information Classification and Handling Policy)

    • For AI systems that are complex or use novel technologies, where risks might not be fully understood.

    On a regular basis for existing AI systems to reassess risks and account for changes or updates.

    A Privacy Impact Assessment (DPIA) should be conducted:¶

    • Whenever an AI system collects, processes, stores, or transmits personal data.

    • When there are significant changes to how personal data is processed, which could affect user privacy.

    • To ensure compliance with data protection laws (UK DPA 2018/EU GDPR).

    • On a regular basis (at every 2 years) for existing AI systems to reassess privacy risks and account for changes or updates.

    Assessments must be reviewed and updated, if needed, at least biannually or whenever the AI system undergoes significant changes. These assessments should be conducted to verify that the implemented measures remain adequate in relation to its security, privacy, and other relevant risks, as well as to proactively identify and mitigate evolving security threats.

    Documentation of controls¶

    For every AI system, the following must be documented:

    • The outcomes of AI Risk Assessments, Privacy Assessments, and Security Impact Assessments.

    • All measures and controls implemented to mitigate identified risks, protect privacy, and ensure security.

    Documentation must be updated:

    • Whenever a new assessment is conducted, or an existing assessment is updated.

    • Following the implementation of any new measures or controls, or significant adjustments to existing ones.

    • To reflect any changes in AI system design, operation, or use that could influence the effectiveness of existing controls.

    Roles and Responsibilities¶

    Introduction¶

    This chapter outlines the roles and responsibilities regarding the design, development, implementation, and use of AI within Community Fibre. .

    An overview of specific actions with specific actions owner can be found in 5.7 Overview of all actions with owners.

    Business Owner¶

    • The respective management responsible for the procurement and initiating the implementation or use of an AI system will be deemed Business Owner.

    • The Business Owner has the following responsibilities:

    • Ensure that objectives for the AI system align with overall business goals.

    • Develop a strategic plan for AI implementation, including timelines and resource allocation.

    • The Business Owner is responsible for overseeing the overall compliance of the AI system with Community Fibre policies, laws, regulations, and industry standards, including the AI Policy and the Data Protection Policy.

    • This role involves ensuring strategic alignment and accountability, providing guidance and support to the Technical Owner, and securing the resources necessary for effective implementation and compliance.

    • Establish accountability for decisions made by the AI system.

    Technical Owner¶

    • The Technical Owner responsible for the development, testing and deployment and maintenance of AI systems, will be deemed as responsible Technical Owner.

    • The Technical Owner has the following responsibilities:

    • Develop, test, and deploy AI models aligned with company objectives.

    • Ensure that AI design requirements are considered.

    • Monitor AI systems for performance and ethical compliance.

    • Manage data quality and ethical sourcing for AI models.

    • Adhere to internal AI ethical guidelines and best practices.

    • Identify and mitigate operational risks in AI systems.

    • Report AI performance and risks to management and stakeholders

    Governance, Risk & Compliance (GRC)¶

    • GRC is the responsibility of the ISMS Board, chaired by the Head of Security

    • The Head of Security will be responsible for:

    • AI Register Maintenance and oversight of AI Intakes

    • Establish policies and guidelines for ethical AI usage.

    • Defining and monitoring of AI Controls.

    • Facilitate risk assessments focusing on ethical, legal, and operational aspects of AI.

    • Support compliance with external regulations and internal AI policies.

    • Provide guidance on AI risk management and ethical considerations.

    • Facilitate training on AI risk, ethics, and compliance.

    • Monitor AI systems and processes for compliance and effectiveness.

    Community Fibre Technical Architecture Team¶

    Community Fibre's Architecture team consists of subject matter experts and collaborate and obtain functional and non-functional requirements from the business. The core team should at least be represented by experts in:

    • IT

    • Data Science

    • Mass market marketing

    • Data Protection

    • Compliance and ethics

    • Legal & Regulatory

    Community Fibre's Architecture team has the following responsibilities:

    • Provide multidisciplinary insights on AI projects, bridging gaps between technical, legal, compliance, and business perspectives.

    • Review AI projects for ethical considerations, including data privacy, fairness, and transparency.

    • Assist in interpreting AI policies in the context of specific projects, ensuring compliance and alignment.

    • Support a cohesive approach to AI implementation and governance.

    End-Users of AI Systems¶

    End-users of AI Systems have the following responsibilities:

    • Provide feedback on AI system usability and effectiveness.

    • Adhere to the Community Fibre AI Policy, guidelines and best practices for AI system usage.

    • Participate in training and awareness programs related to AI.

    • Report issues or concerns regarding AI system performance or ethical implications.
