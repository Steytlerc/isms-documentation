    General Information¶

    Document Objective¶

    This User Registration Policy outlines the requirements for registering and de-registering users at Community Fibre Limited, ensuring secure onboarding and offboarding to protect information assets. It supports the overarching ISP010-Access Control Policy by focusing on user registration and de-registration processes and complements other supporting policies such as theISP011 -- Information Access Rights Policy , ISP040-Password Management Policy, ISP012-Identity Management Policy, and ISP013 -- Privileged Access Rights Policy.

    This policy ensures alignment with business needs, information security risks, and regulatory requirements, including the Electronic Communications (Security Measures) Regulations 2022 and its associated Code of Practice.

    This document meets the following clauses:

    • Information Security Management System ISO/IEC 27001:2022 -- A.5.16: Identity management (user registration aspects)

    • The Electronic Communications (Security Measures) Regulations 2022 -- Regulation 8: Access controls for security-critical functions, including unique credentials and verification

    Policy¶

    Scope and principles¶

    This policy applies to the registration and de-registration of all users, including employees, contractors, third parties, and system/service accounts, who require access to Community Fibre\'s information assets, networks, systems, and services. It covers the full user lifecycle and aligns with joiners, movers, and leavers processes.

    Key principles:

    • Every user must be assigned a unique identifier for accountability.

    • Registration controls are proportionate to the level of access and asset sensitivity (per ISP037-Information Classification and Handling Policy).

    • Initial access is minimal, based on role and need-to-know.

    • Use automation for efficient, error-free registration (ISP012-Identity Management Policy).

    • Ensure immediate removal of access upon departure or change to prevent unauthorised use.

    • All processes are auditable and aligned with legal and regulatory obligations.

    User registration is managed by HR, IT Service Desk, and Security Operations, with oversight from the ISMS Board.

    User registration process¶

    • Registration is initiated by a formal request from an authorised manager or HR for employees, or via contracts for contractors/third parties.

    • The process includes:

    • Obtaining full name, role, contact information, and justification for access.

    • Assignment of a unique user ID, linked to the individual\'s identity (ISP012-Identity Management Policy).

    • Initial access provisioning based on RBAC, aligned with job role and least privilege (ISP011 -- Information Access Rights Policy).

    • Issuance of authentication information, such as temporary passwords or MFA setup (ISP040-Password Management Policy).

    • For privileged access, additional approvals and controls apply (per ISP013 -- Privileged Access Rights Policy).

    • Users must acknowledge acceptance of policies (e.g., ISP030-Acceptable Use Policy.pdf, Code of Conduct) and complete security awareness training within a reasonable timeframe after activation.

    • Automated tools (e.g., integration with Microsoft Entra ID) are used for provisioning to ensure consistency and speed.

    Verification and authentication¶

    • Identity verification is required prior to registration:

    • HR verifies via background checks, ID documents, and right-to-work confirmation.

    • Contracts, NDAs/DPAs, and supplier risk assessments must be completed.

    • Basic for Public/Confidential assets; enhanced (e.g., security vetting) for Strictly Confidential or privileged access.

    • Authentication setup includes:

    • Unique, non-guessable credentials; no defaults.

    • MFA enablement for remote or sensitive access.

    • Risk-based checks (e.g., behavioural analytics) during initial login.

    • Temporary credentials must be changed immediately upon first use.

    User de-registration process¶

    • De-registration is triggered immediately by HR notifications (e.g., termination) or security incidents.

    • The process includes:

    • Revocation of all access rights and credentials (ISP011 -- Information Access Rights Policy).

    • Disablement or deletion of user IDs (ISP012-Identity Management Policy).

    • Return or secure disposal of company assets (e.g., devices, badges).

    • Automated de-provisioning where integrated; manual steps logged and confirmed.

    • Dormant accounts (inactive for 90 days) are automatically disabled and reviewed for permanent removal.

    • Post-de-registration audits ensure no residual access remains.

    Third-party and contractor registration¶

    • Third-party users follow the same processes but with additional controls:

    • Registration requires NDA and approved contracts and specifying access scope, duration, and monitoring.

    • Access is time-bound and limited to specific systems/assets.

    • Remote access restricted to jump boxes or managed devices; UK-based where possible.

    • Regular reviews (e.g., quarterly) to confirm ongoing need.

    • Risks from third parties are assessed and mitigated (e.g., via DPAs for PII handling).v

    Monitoring and review¶

    • All registration and de-registration activities are logged by the IT Service Desk.

    • Periodic reviews (at least every 6 months) validate active users against business needs.

    **

    Regulatory requirements¶

    • User registration for security-critical functions ensures unique credentials, verification, and segregation (Regulation 8(2) (b-c), 8(5)).

    • Remote access is restricted to managed devices (Regulation 4(5)).

    • Third-party risks are assessed (Regulation 11(b)), with logging of registrations (Code of Practice para 3.4-3.7).

    • Training includes competency on access controls (Regulation 13(2)(a)).
