    General Information¶

    This document meets the following clauses:

    • Information Security Management System ISO/IEC 27001:2022 -- A.5.10 Acceptable use of information and other associated assets

    What we do¶

    The purpose of this control is to ensure information and other associated assets are appropriately protected, used and handled. Special consideration is given to privacy and the protection of personally identifiable information (PII).

    The acceptable use of our information and associated assets is a fundamental principle and first layer of security within the organisation, whilst ensuring flexibility of working.

    To this end we expect anyone who has access to our information and systems to:

    • Comply with the Acceptable Use Policy (below);

    • Comply with other information security policies stored in Policies for information security;

    • Comply with Information Classification and Handling Policy;

    • Comply with the Code of Conduct; 

    • Understand that anything defined as unacceptable or non-compliant with the policies in the ISMS or the Code of Conduct may be dealt with in accordance with Disciplinary process.

    Occasional, personal use of Community Fibre's assets is permitted providing the expectations above are adhered to.

    Wherever possible Community Fibre avoids the creation of paper-based information assets itself and attempts to produce only digital information.

    Customers and others who receive our information can print and share as they see fit based on their policies and procedures in accordance with the classification and any controls we have applied to the information.

    If you accessed a malicious email, link, attachment or website or you have any other security concerns, please contact the IT Service Desk or Security Operations immediately.

    IT Service Desk - itsupport@communityfibre.co.uk

    Security Operations - security@communityfibre.co.uk

    Policy¶

    The purpose of this policy is to set out the limited set of rules to which personnel must adhere to when using Community Fibre's systems, information, networks, and other related assets. It is also designed to protect employees from accidentally breaching legislation.

    The Acceptable Use Policy does not seek to impose restrictions that are contrary to our culture of openness, trust, and integrity.

    Senior management is committed to protecting Community Fibre's employees, customers, and the organisation itself from illegal or damaging actions by individuals, whether deliberate or accidental.

    Policy Statements¶

    General Use & Ownership¶

    1. All networks and systems, including but not limited to network and computer equipment, software, operating systems, storage media, and login accounts are the property of Community Fibre.

    2. Community Fibre systems shall be used primarily for business purposes in serving the interests of Community Fibre and of our customers during normal operations.

    3. Organisational data shall be transferred, processed, or stored on approved Community Fibre business systems.

    4. Occasional personal use of Community Fibre equipment and access to the Internet is permitted providing such use does not amount to "unacceptable use" defined below.

    5. Because of the need to protect Community Fibre\'s network and business systems, management cannot guarantee the confidentiality of personal information stored on any device or service belonging to Community Fibre.

    6. Employees are responsible for exercising good judgment regarding the reasonableness of personal use.

    7. If there is any uncertainty, employees must consult their supervisor or manager.

    8. Wherever possible, any information considered confidential or sensitive must be encrypted in line with the Cryptographic Control Policy

    9. For security and network maintenance purposes, authorised individuals within the organisation may monitor equipment, systems, and network traffic at any time.

    10. The organisation reserves the right to audit networks and systems on a periodic basis to ensure compliance with this policy.

    11. Employees must take all necessary steps to prevent unauthorised access to information.

    12. All employees and contractors that in any way collect, process, store or handle Community Fibre Limited data are required to follow the Information Classification and Handling Policy

    13. Employees and other authorised users:

      a. must keep passwords and other security authentication information secure and not share accounts.

      b. are responsible for the security of their passwords and accounts in line with the Password Management Policy.

    14. All end user devices must be secured with a password-protected screensaver with the automatic activation feature set at 10 minutes or less.

    15. Users must secure their devices whenever the device will be left unattended.

    16. Special care must be taken to protect mobile devices.

    17. Posts by employees from a Community Fibre email address on social media must contain a disclaimer stating that the opinions expressed are strictly their own and not necessarily those of the organisation unless posting is during business duties.

    18. All equipment used by the employee that is connected to Community Fibre's business systems, whether owned by the employee or the organisation, shall be continually executing approved virus-scanning software with a current virus database unless overridden by IT group policy.

    19. Employees must use extreme caution before opening e-mail attachments received from unknown senders as these may contain viruses, e-mail bombs, or Trojan horse code.

    If you accessed a malicious email, link, attachment or website or you have any other security concerns, please contact the IT Service Desk or Security Operations immediately.

    IT Service Desk - itsupport@communityfibre.co.uk

    Security Operations - security@communityfibre.co.uk

    Unacceptable Use¶

    The following activities are prohibited. Employees may be exempted from some of these restrictions during the course of their legitimate job responsibilities.

    The lists below are by no means exhaustive but attempt to provide a framework for activities which fall into the category of unacceptable use.

    Unacceptable System and Network Activities¶

    The following activities are strictly prohibited, with no exceptions:

    1. Any activity that is illegal under relevant local, national, or international legislation.

    2. Violations of the rights of any person or company protected by copyright, trade secret, patent or other intellectual property, or similar laws or regulations, including, but not limited to, the installation or distribution of \"pirated\" or other software products that are not appropriately licensed for use by the organisation.

    3. The transfer, processing or storage of Community Fibre data on personal or otherwise unapproved software systems. (i.e., personal messaging applications (i.e. WhatsApp), personal email or cloud storage, etc.)

    4. The exporting of software, technical information, or cryptographic technology, in violation of international or regional export control laws.

    5. Intentional introduction of malicious programs into the network or servers (e.g., malware, untrusted code libraries)

    6. Revealing your account password to others or allowing use of your account by others. This includes family and other household members when work is being done at home.

    7. Using a Community Fibre device or service to actively engage in procuring or transmitting material that is in violation of sexual harassment or hostile workplace laws in the user\'s local jurisdiction.

    8. Making fraudulent offers of products, items, or services originating from any Community Fibre account.

    9. Making statements about warranty, expressly or implied, unless it is a part of normal job duties.

    10. Effecting security breaches or disruptions of network communication - Security breaches include, but are not limited to, accessing data to which the employee is not an intended recipient or logging into a server or account that the employee is not expressly authorised to access, unless these duties are within the scope of the job role. For purposes of this section, \"disruption\" includes, but is not limited to, network packet capture, any type of denial-of-service activity, packet forgery and forged routing information for malicious purposes.

    11. Port scanning or security scanning is expressly prohibited unless prior notification is made to and authorised by the Chief Information Officer or Operations Director.

    12. Executing any form of network monitoring which will intercept data not intended for the employee\'s host unless this activity is a part of the employee\'s normal job role.

    13. Circumventing user authentication or security of any host, device, network, or account.

    14. Interfering with or denying service to any user other than the employee\'s host (for example by executing a denial-of-service attack).

    15. Using any program/script/command, or sending messages of any kind, with the intent to interfere with, or disable, a user\'s session, via any means, locally or via the Internet/Intranet/Extranet.

    16. Providing information about, or lists of, the organisation's employees to parties outside of the organisation.

    17. Storage of bulk data such as personal video or music files on organisational equipment.

    18. Storage of any company information on USB devices are not acceptable, unless expressly exempted.

    Unacceptable Email and Communications Activities¶

    1. Sending unsolicited email messages, including the sending of \"junk mail\" or other advertising material to individuals who did not specifically request such material (email spam).

    2. Any form of harassment via email, telephone, or messaging whether through language, frequency, or size of messages.

    3. Unauthorised use, or forging, of email header information.

    4. Solicitation of email for any other email address, other than that of the poster\'s account, with the intent to harass or to collect replies.

    5. Creating or forwarding \"chain letters\", \"Ponzi\" or other \"pyramid\" schemes of any type.

    6. Use of unsolicited email originating from within Community Fibre's networks or other Internet/Intranet/Extranet service providers on behalf of, or to advertise, any service hosted by Community Fibre or connected via Community Fibre\'s network.

    Consequences of non-compliance with this policy¶

    Any non-compliance with or breach of policy may lead to investigation and action in line with the organisational Disciplinary Policy in the CFL Employee Handbook. (Community Fibre - Employee Handbook 2023 - v3)
