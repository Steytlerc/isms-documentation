    General Information¶

    1.1 Document Objective¶

    This policy sets out remote working security measures for activities within scope of the Information Security Management System (ISMS) and serves as the primary point of reference.

    1.2 Scope¶

    This document meets the following clauses:

    • Information Security Management System BS EN ISO/IEC 27001:2022:

    A.5.23 Information security for use of cloud services

    A.6.7 Remote working

    A.8.1 User endpoint devices

    A.7.10 Storage media

    Mobile devices and teleworking¶

    The Mobile Device and Teleworking Policy has been implemented to allow personnel to work away from Community Fibre office locations in a secure manner.

    Personally owned devices may be used subject to prior approval and conditions.

    Community Fibre sets the controls applicable to business information stored or processed by mobile devices based on the information classification set out in ISP037-Information Classification and Handling Policy.

    2.1 Mobile device policy¶

    The purpose of this policy is to set out the rules for mobile working and the use of mobile devices. This policy applies to all mobile devices including laptops.

    Use of mobile devices¶

    1. Personally owned (Bring Your Own Device - BYOD) mobile devices can be used to access Community Fibre Limited business information systems or to store or process company information by registering the device and installing approved apps, such as Island Browser, that enable secure corporate access within a dedicated work profile.

    2. All devices that process our information, whether provided by Community Fibre or personally owned, will automatically be recorded on company mobile management systems upon registration.

    3. Work profiles on personally owned devices will be used to separate personal data and applications from corporate ones, ensuring that company information remains isolated and secure.

    4. Information must be removed from mobile devices in line with the ISP037-Information Classification and Handling Policy.

    5. Personally owned mobile devices shall not be used for access to security-critical functions and must comply with additional controls, including restricted internet access and enhanced monitoring.

    Technical requirements¶

    1. Devices must use the most recent stable version of the operating system (OS) available.

    2. Remote wipe capabilities must be enabled if available. BYOD scenarios will enable the deletion of company data only.

    3. Anti-virus and anti-malware software approved by Security Operations shall be enabled. Endpoint detection and response (EDR) tools shall be deployed if proportionate and where possible.

    4. Devices must not be "jailbroken" or have any software/firmware installed which is designed to gain access to functionality not intended to be exposed to the user.

    5. Devices must automatically lock after no more than 10 minutes of inactivity, ideally less. This applies to BYOD scenarios.

    6. Devices must store all user-saved passwords in an encrypted password store.

    7. If a password or passcode is used to unlock devices, it must meet organisational standards for length and complexity (minimum 8 characters, including uppercase, lowercase and numbers).

    8. If a password manager is used, then:

      a. The password manager must require the entry of another password or passcode before auto-filling forms with authentication credentials.

      b. To access the password vault fully, the user must enter a password that meets organisational requirements for master passwords.

      c. Refer to ISP040-Password Management Policy for guidance.

    9. Multi-factor authentication (MFA) must be enabled for all remote access to company systems.

    10. Remote access to the core network or security-critical functions shall use a secure tunnelling protocol with encryption approved by Security Operations, and access shall not be permitted from countries listed in the Regulations Schedule.

    11. Devices must be configured with a firewall blocking incoming traffic.

    User requirements¶

    1. Only load data essential to your role onto your mobile device(s).

    2. You must report all lost or stolen devices to the IT Service Desk immediately.

    3. If you suspect that unauthorised access to company data has taken place via a mobile device, you must report the incident to the IT Service Desk or Security Operations immediately.

    4. You must not load pirated software or illegal content onto any company devices.

    5. Applications installations must be approved and from official platform-owner approved sources. Installation of code from untrusted sources is forbidden.

    6. Devices must be kept up to date with manufacturer provided software updates. As a minimum, updates must be checked weekly and applied at least once a month.

    7. Users shall not disable security controls on mobile devices, as per the Acceptable Use Policy.

    2.2 Management of removable media¶

    Given that most of our information is stored in cloud-based services, to be accessed on authorised devices anywhere, there should be little need to transfer information using removable media. USB storage will be restricted to most staff; exceptions may be approved by Security Operations upon request.

    Where removable media is necessary or practical it must be done in accordance with our policy (below). The purpose of this policy is to define the rules by which removable media (e.g., a USB stick) may be used.

    1. Use of removable media must be in accordance with the ISP037-Information Classification and Handling Policy

    2. The use of USB storage devices will be monitored.

    3. USB storage devices may automatically prompt encryption to be enabled before you are able to use it.

    4. Data stored in removable storage will be removed when no longer needed.

    2.3 Disposal of media¶

    As Community Fibre mostly works from home, typical secure disposal facilities are not available. The use of removable storage and other physical media should therefore be avoided and restricted where possible. Information that is disposed of must be protected from breaches of confidentiality through correct erasure/decommissioning/destruction processes whenever it is to be reused or reaches its end of life.

    Paper records generated at home that contains Confidential or Strictly Confidential information must be securely destroyed, and under no circumstance be placed in the household or communal rubbish bin. Paper records can be sent to the Community Fibre Office where IT Service Desk will arrange for secure destruction.

    Please refer to the ISP044-Secure Disposal Policy for guidance.

    2.4 Physical media transfer¶

    Physical media containing information must be protected against unauthorised access, misuse, or corruption during transportation. It includes physical media itself and devices which contain physical media, such as laptops when they are being transported without their regular user protecting them e.g., during repairs or other reasons.

    General considerations¶

    Only reputable, recognised brands (e.g., Royal Mail, Gopher, DHL) and reliable couriers may be used for transporting physical media held on devices. Protection guarantees must be sought based on the classification of the information.

    In accordance with ISP037-Information Classification and Handling Policy, "Confidential" or "Strictly Confidential" information must be encrypted on the media which provides a good level of protection in the event of theft or misplacement of the device.

    Controls for multi-factor authentication (MFA) oriented devices and media¶

    • Hard token:

    • the MFA device must not be stored or transferred with the device it unlocks (e.g., a case containing a laptop must not also contain the MFA device); and

    • when an MFA device is sent by post or courier it must not be sent in the same package as the device it unlocks

    • Soft token:

    • the MFA processing device must not be the same device for processing information (e.g., the same smart phone) unless the device itself requires an unlock passcode or biometric data.

    Lost device process¶

    The following steps must be taken immediately:

    1. Lost devices must be reported to the IT Service Desk (itsupport@communityfibre.co.uk) immediately.

    2. An investigation carried out to establish:

      a. What assets are contained on the media.

      b. What protection of those assets is in place. (e.g., encryption)

    3. Remedial action that may include:

      a. De-authorising the device for any services that offer a remote reset and removal of access privileges.

      b. Revoking any tokens on the device

      c. Changing affected passwords

      d. A remote wipe of the whole device

    2.9 Clear desk and clear screen policy¶

    A clear desk policy for paper and removable storage media and a clear screen policy for information processing facilities have been implemented.

    The clear desk and clear screen policy consider:

    • Classification of information (ISP037-Information Classification and Handling Policy); and

    • Identification of applicable legislation and contractual requirements (A.5.31)

    Clear Desk¶

    1. "Strictly confidential" information must not be left alone or exposed to unauthorised users at any point.

    2. "Confidential" information on paper or electronic storage media, must be secured when not needed, especially when the office is vacated (ideally in paperless form in the first instance, otherwise locked in the desk pedestal, filing cabinet or other furniture at home or in the office)

    3. Incoming and outgoing mail points are monitored by facilities management staff.

    4. Documents containing "Confidential", or "Strictly confidential" information must be removed from printers immediately.

    Clear Screen¶

    1. Devices must have a lock screen enabled.

    2. There is no need to physically secure devices if the room in which the device is held has adequate security (e.g., door locks, or it is within trusted premises)

    3. If there is any concern for safety, then employees must take their device with them.

    4. Care must be taken to protect Confidential or Strictly Confidential when in public areas as data may be disclosed inadvertently or intentionally. Be careful where you take a call that covers sensitive information and try to position yourself in a busy area so that your device screen cannot be seen, or you cannot be overheard.

    2.10 Controls against malware¶

    We empower users by giving the freedom to access external information online without restriction or lockdown of sites if they stay within the boundaries of our Acceptable Use Policy and Terms of Employment.

    We also encourage them to stay abreast of general security best-practice procedures and precautions. Staff communications and awareness on the subject is also assured through the ISMS Board in line with Information security awareness, training, and education. Given these controls and those below we do not employ any other explicit form of software allow or block listing.

    Deliberate actions taken by users to deactivate anti-malware controls (e.g., anti-virus software) is specifically noted as "Unacceptable use" in our Acceptable Use Policy and may lead to investigation and possible use of the Disciplinary Process.

    For devices used by employees¶

    The use of anti-virus software on employee devices detects and eliminates the vast majority of common malware that is in existence.

    We use well-known and widely trusted products to minimise the risk that a user will encounter malware.

    Malware in documents¶

    Documents may contain malware in the form of macros, for example macros in Word or Excel documents. Caution must be exercised when opening documents that contain macros. We need to check that the received document:

    • Is sent by a known or trusted source.

    • Is expected or is likely to be something that the sender would normally send out (i.e., the sender\'s computer hasn\'t been compromised by malware which sends out infected documents)

    • Attack surface reduction policies will be employed to reduce or remove the risk of malware executed from documents.

    Where there is doubt the recipient must contact the sender of the document to obtain confirmation before opening the file.

    Suspected or actual compromise¶

    • If a user suspects malware may have executed (including any unwanted software) they must raise a security event through the IT Service Desk (itsupport@communityfibre.co.uk) and/or Security Operations (security@communityfibre.co.uk).

    Consequences of non-compliance with this policy¶

    Any non-compliance with or breach of policy may lead to investigation and action in line with the organisational Disciplinary Policy (Community Fibre - Employee Handbook 2023 - v3).
