    General Information¶

    Document Objective¶

    The purpose of this document is to set out our approach to risk assessment and treatment regarding compliance with ISO 27001:2022 and other standards as required. It also draws on practices within risk management focused ISO 31000. The policy reflects how we run our normal day-to-day business from a risk perspective, using our core tool for delivery: The ISO 27001 Risk Register and Treatment Plan (ISMS.online)

    ISO 27001:2022 is very clear that the approach towards addressing risks and opportunities is about information security. The ISO 27001 Risk Register and Treatment Plan tool within the ISMS.online platform is used to record and assess both.

    Scope¶

    This document meets the following clauses:

    • Information Security Management System BS EN ISO/IEC 27001:2017 -- 8.2 Information security risk assessment

    Risk identification¶

    In terms of risk identification and initial inputs into the risk analysis we do the following:

    1. Consider the organisation and its context (4.1) including Internal and External issues.

    2. Understand the needs and expectations of Interested Parties (4.2) taking their importance into account from their position on the stakeholder map including any applicable legislation, regulation, or contractual requirement (18.1.1), along with the scope of the system (4.3).

    3. Consider ISO 27001 Annex A policy and controls areas to help stimulate further identification of risk in the above areas, as well as review compliance regimes adopted within the organisation such as GDPR, Cyber Essentials and The Electronic Communications Security Measures Regulations 2022.

    The risk identification work may be achieved through brainstorming / workshop facilitation, desktop research and business plan review, asset register inventory, existing process documentation review etc. 4.1 and 4.2 policies also suggest cursory Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability impact assessment for whether internal and external issues and interested parties may lead to risks that need more detailed analysis, evaluation and treatment described below.

    New risks are assessed as they become known to the organisation e.g., through project work in accordance with 5.8, management reviews, or other interactions with Interested Parties or general research.

    Adding and updating risks¶

    Having identified a risk from the process above, the relevantly worded risk is added to the ISO 27001 risk register and treatment plan within ISMS.online.

    The following principles apply:

    • Risks are relevantly described with a simple reference for whether they are internal, external or both.

    • If not clear from the risk description, a specific source and or actor can optionally be added e.g. People, Organisation, Products & Services, Process, Asset, PESTLE analysis, or Interested Party to assist in any future root cause analysis.

    • Each risk has one overall owner but can have individual treatment actions delivered by the same or other people and tracked in that risk area.

    • All work around risks is audited. It is down to the relevant risk owner to demonstrate enough evidence to auditors and internal stakeholders that appropriate action has been taken.

    • Risks can be identified, added, and updated within the tool at any time by one of the Information Security Management System Board (ISMS Board).

    • Risks are reviewed at varying intervals based on their position on the map and other factors for consideration, including changes in internal or external factors or the requirements of interested parties; legislation or regulation changes; or changes in the threat landscape.

    • Any changes will be drawn to the attention of other ISMS Board members and team members of the tool via their updates feed, and if appropriate through the normal management review process.

    Any significant changes affecting Interested Parties will be communicated and addressed using the relevant change control and communication mechanisms. These include section 6 of ISO 27001 for Resources, Competence, Awareness and Communication, and others Interested Parties for example through (5.31) applicable legislation, and (5.20) suppliers.

    Assessing risks¶

    A recognised process of likelihood and impact analysis is considered for risk assessment, considering the potential loss of confidentiality, integrity and availability of information for the impact scoring.

    The descriptions in the table below are considered when conducting risk likelihood and impact analysis. N.B. these are written from the perspective of threat-based risks as these are the most common risks we will assess. Opportunity risks, when considered, are simply to be viewed through the mirror of the threat-based descriptions below e.g., where it says damage/consequence it may be a benefit instead.

    Likelihood:

    Level Description

    Very low No history of occurrence and the event may only happen in exceptional circumstances with a high level of investment and or specialist skills

    Low The event has not occurred before but could do so without exceptional circumstances being needed or high level of investment and or specialist skills

    Medium The event has occurred in the past and could recur without exceptional circumstances being needed or high level of investment and or specialist skills

    High The event will occur at some point and may be a repeat of past events

    Very High There is a history of regular reoccurrence

    Impact:

    The impact level is automatically determined by taking the highest value from the Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability (CIA) analysis that is undertaken for each risk. The tables below present the CIA descriptions.

    Confidentiality and Integrity:

    Level Description

    None The risk does not affect this area

    Insignificant (i.e., Very Low) - negligible or no damage to business operation and reputation with no costs or consequences

    Minor Low damage to business operation or reputation with negligible costs or consequences

    Moderate Some damage or loss to business operation or reputation with some cost exposure but not affecting future prospects

    Major Serious but not complete damage to business operation or reputation with large cost exposure and affecting future prospects

    Severe Severe damage to business operation or reputation with very large cost exposure and leaving no future prospects

    Availability:

    Score Description

    None The risk does not affect this area

    Insignificant Can be absorbed and shows no measurable impact - no service disruption

    Minor Work distraction; minor increases in support or costs - low disruption to service

    Moderate Work delays; noticeable impact to costs and productivity - short disruption to service

    Major Work interruption; business commitments delayed with serious costs

    Severe Work stoppage and extended service closure; substantial support costs

    Confidentiality - information is not made available or disclosed to unauthorised individuals, entities, or processes, i.e., it means ensuring that information held is only accessible to those who are authorized to see it (within a defined group of users). A breach of confidentiality is where the information concerned is disclosed, maliciously or accidentally, and can be assumed to come into the hands of unauthorised persons. This also encompasses accidental misrouting to an unintended party.

    Integrity - safeguarding the accuracy and completeness of information. Ensuring it is a sound representation of authorised business processes or people. Integrity addresses the following aspects: completeness, accuracy, validity, maintenance of stored information including timeliness of updates. A breach of integrity is where the information concerned is deliberately or accidentally altered; it has arrived out of sequence, or its integrity has otherwise been compromised during transaction; or it has been duplicated or deleted.

    Availability - being accessible and usable upon demand by an authorised entity, i.e., meaning ensuring that information and/or service is available when required for business operations. A breach of availability is the inability to transact in expected or contracted time-scales due to flaws in the process or because of a disaster or failure.

    Treating risks¶

    The ISO 27001 Risk Register & Treatment Plan tool within the ISMS platform works on a matrix-based method. The risk position on the matrix is determined by likelihood and impact. The grid position is then reflected with a unique number from 1-25 for ease of identification.

    The higher the number, the greater the overall risk; with impact being considered more important than likelihood. Monitoring and treatment are determined based on the position in the matrix and detail below:

    {width="4.08578302712161in" height="4.088578302712161in"}

    Risks are updated dynamically when required and reviewed in accordance with their position in the grid and reflecting the overall level of risk as follows:

    a) For map positions in the green (1-5): at least annually

    b) For map positions in the yellow (6-14): at least six monthly

    c) For map positions in the red (15-20): at least quarterly

    d) For map positions in the black (21-25): at least monthly

    The online risk treatment assessment and treatment plan allows users to select relevant dates for receiving reminders and managing their risks in good time to avoid risk reviews going overdue and creating further risk in the organisation.

    The treatment plan is maintained in ISMS.online and any activities such as IT changes as part of risk treatment activities are logged in Jira as a change request.

    The risk owner is responsible for identifying the appropriate treatment and considering the criteria for accepting (tolerating) the risk as follows:

    • Terminate - the aim being to remove the risk entirely.

    • Treat - the aim being to reduce its impact and/or likelihood to more acceptable levels.

    • Transfer - the aim being to transfer or share the risk e.g., to or with a supplier.

    • Tolerate - i.e., accept the residual risk.

    • Combination of actions - taking one or more the treatment options above

    If there are conflicts in the mapping and treatment of risks and decisions to be made in the way we go about addressing them, the following will always take priority for impact decisions on confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information:

    a. Any legal, regulatory, or contractual requirement identified from a risk perspective would trump any desire for internal business improvement or further growth.

    b. Where one or more stakeholders are specifically impacted by a potential conflict in risk management, we may choose to take that to the stakeholders (as an Interested Party) and consider appropriate courses of action with them. As part of that process, risk treatment may involve them accepting that risk or helping us treat that risk e.g., by transferring it, sharing it, or co-operating on the removal of it.

    Risk acceptance criteria¶

    In general, we are happy to accept risks shown as in the green area on the risk map and as the risk position increases into the yellow, red and black areas the less likely we are to accept the risk without further treatment.

    The final risk acceptance criteria are determined by the risk owner, but should take into consideration the following:

    • The level of residual risk to be accepted.

    • Any legal, regulatory, or contractual obligations

    • Business objectives and internal compliance requirements

    • Other conflicting risks that may be introduced or changed from further treating the risk being considered (this may include e.g., risks to resourcing levels and/or financial implications)

    The residual risk being tolerated (i.e., the risk remaining after acting) is demonstrated from its position on the risk map and this position will also determine how often the risk is to be reviewed (see above).

    Process for applicable legislation & regulation-based risk assessment and treatment¶

    Whilst ISO 27001 does not require the assessment of risks relating to each individual piece of legislation, regulation or contractual requirement, the use of the ISMS.online risk tool to record these enables this additional assessment. The process for this is as follows:

    1. Identify applicable legislation and regulation affecting the organisation.

    2. Add the applicable legislation and regulation into the Applicable Legislation risk register & treatment plan online tool.

    3. Describe its applicability and relevance (keeping it simple).

    4. Conduct a likelihood and impact analysis on the risk for the organisation. Where the following is for consideration in the mapping and management:

      a. Likelihood is the likelihood of a breach affecting compliance with the legislation, regulation or contractual compliance including how likely it is that the legislation will change.

      b. Impact is the impact of a breach of the legislation and the effects of that legislation coming down on you (or your interested parties because of your behaviour) such that your business has negative consequences.

    Likelihood:

    Score Description

    Very low The legislation has relevance for you or interested parties who rely on your services to ensure compliance but having complied, the legislation and controls to meet it are very unlikely to change or influence daily business. It has very low day to day management and monitoring.

    Low The legislation has relevance for you or interested parties who rely on your services to ensure compliance but having complied, the legislation and controls to meet it are unlikely to change or influence daily business. It has low day to day management and monitoring.

    Medium The legislation has relevance for you or interested parties who rely on your services to ensure compliance. Having complied, the legislation and controls to meet it are unlikely to change or influence daily business but could do so given their nature. It has medium attention for management and monitoring.

    High The legislation has relevance for you or interested parties who rely on your services to ensure compliance. Having complied, the legislation and controls to meet it are highly likely to change or influence daily business given their nature. It has high attention for management and monitoring.

    Very High The legislation has relevance for you or interested parties who rely on your services to ensure compliance. Having complied, the legislation and controls or practices to meet it are very highly likely to change or influence daily business given their nature. It has very high attention for management and monitoring.

    Impact:

    Score Description

    Very low Negligible or no damage to business operation and reputation with no costs or consequences

    Low Low damage to business operation or reputation with negligible costs or consequences

    Medium Some damage or loss to business operation or reputation with some cost exposure but not affecting future prospects

    High Serious but not complete damage to business operation or reputation with large cost exposure and affecting prospects

    Very High Severe damage to business operation or reputation with very large cost exposure and leaving no prospects
