### Introduction {#introduction .Heading-3_Green}

The purpose of this document is to provide an overview of Community
Fibre Limited, the activities it conducts and the ISMS standards of
operation it conforms to.

Community Fibre Limited handles staff, vendor, corporate, customer and
customer personnel data and the business must be able to demonstrate
that information security has been integrated into processes, and that
all information is managed securely.

Certification to ISO 27001:2022 of the Community Fibre Limited ISMS will
provide assurance internally to management and stakeholders, as well as
to customer and other interested parties, that its information is
managed in a controlled and secure manner, and that Community Fibre
Limited is committed to providing a reliable level of service.

The company is committed to meeting regulatory and compliance
obligations, including the UK GDPR/Data Protection Act 2018 and the
Telecommunications (Security) Act 2021. Compliance with these
regulations ensures that Community Fibre maintains high standards of
assurance, resilience, and accountability.

The ISMS is designed to align with ISO 27001:2022 and any standard
adopting the Annex SL structure.

### Overview of the organisation {#overview-of-the-organisation .Heading-3_Green}

Founded in 2010, Community Fibre provides high-speed broadband across
Greater London and Surrey through its fully owned and operated optical
fibre network.

The company is committed to serving multi and single-dwelling,
businesses and increasingly wholesale partners. Community Fibre offers
competitive pricing, exceptional customer support, and high-speed,
reliable, full-fibre connectivity. Its dedication to customer service
has earned it one of the highest Trustpilot ratings among UK ISPs.

Community Fibre provides:

- Fibre broadband services to residential and business customers.

- Fibre network wholesale services to other and often larger UK telecoms
  businesses.

- Support and billing services for residential and business customers.

### Purposes {#purposes .Heading-3_Green}

The purpose of the ISMS and information security program is to protect
the organisation, network and business infrastructure, and the staff and
customer information it collects, processes, and stores.

This is achieved through the documented policies and procedures,
education and awareness initiatives, and appropriate and proportionate
technical and organisational controls. The scope of the ISMS encompasses
core network services and business systems and operations.

Community Fibre recognises the critical value and importance of securing
the information and network assets that drive our business. The ISMS
supports our key strategic aims of:

**Excellent service** -- Security continuity (the practice of keeping
Community Fibre's data and systems protected and operational, no matter
what disruptions hit us) differentiates our services from other
organisations, exceeds customer expectations, and enables us to operate
effectively in an increasingly regulated environment.

**Network and information asset protection** -- Through this ISMS, we
aim to minimise risks associated with malicious activities, accidental
damage, or disruptions to information assets and network infrastructure,
regardless of their form (digital, physical, or otherwise).

**Regulatory and contractual requirements** - Drives compliance with
relevant statutory regulations, including the Telecommunications
(Security) Act 2021 and UK GDPR/Data Protection Act 2018, as well as
contractual requirements.

##### Determining the scope of the information security management system

**The scope of the ISMS is defined as**

Community Fibre Limited operating within Greater London, providing
broadband services to residential, business and wholesale customers; the
core network infrastructure and UK customer data processing within its
business software systems, supported by Customer Services, Network
Technology, HR, Commercial, Finance, Marketing, Operations, Information
Technology, Information Security and Data Protection.

**Exclusions to the ISMS scope**

The boundary of the ISMS Scope can be further identified by the
exclusion of these specific activities:

- The Community Fibre Limited lower tier exchanges, street cabinets,
  ducting, poles; training, testing and equipment and hardware storage
  facilities.

- The physical security of non-Community Fibre Limited locations (e.g.,
  customer sites, private homes) is out of scope of the ISMS. However,
  other security measures applied to Community Fibre Limited Information
  Assets that are used in these locations (e.g., laptops, mobiles)
  remain within scope.

**Detailed Scope description**

The ISMS scope covers Community Fibre\'s secure data handling, core
network infrastructure, and aggregation sites, aligning with regulatory
requirements. The Statement of Applicability details selected controls
for risk treatment, justifying inclusion of all 93 Annex A controls from
ISO 27001:2022. Figure 1 summarises the scope and governance structure.

**The ISMS objectives apply to all in-scope elements. There is mandatory
compliance with the ISMS within this scope.**

**Infrastructure**

**In scope:**

1.  Infrastructure in the Applications zone (Zone 1) includes:

a\. Applications and containers running code that process Community
Fibre Limited data.

b\. Interfaces, such as web or reporting services for accessing,
querying, or downloading data.

c\. Source code

2.  Infrastructure in the Data Services zone (Zone 2), including
    database services, and the software and applications in each virtual
    compute instance.

3.  Infrastructure in the Vendors zone (Zone 3) including authentication
    configuration and company data stored and transferred in or out each
    SaaS service.

4.  Infrastructure in the Data handling zone (Zone 4) includes cloud
    data storage, cloud-based query logic such as APIs, and including
    the any endpoint devices that store company data, and any other
    services that store company data.

5.  Infrastructure in Data handling zone (Zone 5), used to securely
    deliver data or services.

6.  Infrastructure in the Core network and technology zone (Zone 6),
    used to deliver and manage broadband services to customer premises.

7.  Infrastructure in the Identity zone (Zone 7), used to authenticate
    and authorise users, vendors and customers and grant access to
    approved data and services when required.

The above zones will be referred to in all ISMS documentation as the
"Community Fibre infrastructure".

1.  Customer premises.

2.  Lower tier exchanges and hardware, street cabinets and hardware,
    ducting, poles.

    Longwood. Network equipment stored at Longwood are configured at
    point of deployment and restored to factory default or wiped before
    returned to Longwood.

**People**

**In scope:**

1.  All staff members directly employed by Community Fibre Limited.

    service providers that have access to Community Fibre infrastructure
    for example CCI South Africa, TCS, Cognizant and Orange Moldova.

3.  IT and support staff and contractors working on the Community Fibre
    in-scope infrastructure. Contracts, service, and operating level
    agreements must accord with the ISMS.

4.  Suppliers and data providers who enter a contractual agreement with
    Community Fibre Limited.

**Out of scope:**

Users, support staff and data providers while they are not interacting
with Community Fibre infrastructure or data.

**Services**

**In scope:**

Services delivered on Community Fibre infrastructure can be summarised
as data capture, process, access, and storage services, including:

1.  Data captured, processed, and stored through the public company
    website.

2.  All data acquisition activities involving external third parties.

3.  All data enrichment activities involving external third parties.

4.  All transfers of data to and from external third parties.

5.  All internal software development activities.

##### Resources

Community Fibre Limited determines and provides the resources needed for
the establishment, implementation, maintenance, and continual
improvement of the management system. This is evidenced by:

- Leadership resource commitment expressed in
  ([5.1](#_5.1_Leadership_and)) and ([9.3](#management-review))

- Providing the resources for the ISMS per (A.5.3 - Information security
  roles and responsibilities)

- Activity owners and supporting team members for each one of the
  requirements, policies, and controls for the standards we have chosen
  to apply.

- Procurement and creation of the
  [ISMS.online](https://communityfibre.isms.online/projects/18216/structure)
  environment for overall policy control and the enabling infrastructure
  to deliver work such as ISMS Board reviews per
  ([9.3](#management-review)), Internal Audits per
  [9.2](#internal-audit), Nonconformities and Corrective Actions per
  ([10.](#continual-improvement)2) and Risk Assessment & Treatment per
  ([6.1](#_6.1_Actions_to)).

- Specific resources allocated for risk management
  ([6.1](#_6.1_Actions_to)), internal audits ([9.2](#internal-audit)),
  nonconformities, corrective actions, performance evaluation and
  ongoing improvement and specialist areas of work such as managing
  information security incidents.

- Mechanisms for efficiently communicating and engaging all staff and
  supply chain resources in the scope of the management system such as
  staff induction sessions, security awareness training and
  communications via channels such as BambooHR and email.

- Resource investment in the ISMS.online management system platform for
  the professional implementation and ongoing delivery of the management
  system to ensure that our limited physical resources are optimised and
  focused.

Our resources involved in the establishment, implementation,
maintenance, and continual improvement in the management system are
senior experienced staff, experts in their fields. They have all read
around the subject matter for each standard, hold certifications where
appropriate and recognise the importance of staying up to date with
developments in their respective fields. This includes training and
development as required, both informally e.g., online personal
development, and formal training programs with certification at the end.

Harnessing best practice tools such as ISMS.online with built-in
competence for related areas e.g., risk assessment and supplier
management are also evidenced throughout the management system.

The organisation's culture and values push us towards continual
improvement. Information security is a thread like commercial
development or organisation change that we constantly work on in our day
jobs. This is also evidenced where we take the opportunity to further
improve practices by following up research from staff, staying abreast
of what works in the firm and elsewhere.

##### Communication

This section describes formal communications regarding information
security that relates to elements within scope of the ISMS. We need to
ensure that relevant issues (i.e., new policies or significant changes)
are communicated to stakeholders clearly and consistently so that people
have the necessary information and guidance to carry out their
responsibilities for information security.

##### Recipients and triggers

Information security management communications are sent to all
stakeholders who are directly affected by the matter being communicated
or with responsibilities for any affected procedures.

##### Communication Scope

Information security management communications of new and updated
policies should be communicated in a manner that is clear and
comprehensive and may include some of the following:

1.  The purpose or objective of the policy.

2.  Description of the policy as it relates to the recipient.

3.  Responsibilities for implementing and managing the policy.

4.  Feasible timeframe for implementation.

5.  Review plan for the policy.

6.  Opportunity for queries and comments.

However, information must not be distributed where doing so may cause a
compromise to information security.

##### Communication Responsibilities

Effective communications about information security are assigned to the
following roles:

##### Senior Management Security Representative (CIO)

1.  To communicate the importance of effective information security
    management and of conforming to ISMS requirements, and the
    consequences of not doing so.

2.  To review communication policies for making information available to
    relevant people in a timely manner and via appropriate channels.

3.  To ensure the ISMS Board has the relevant information.

4.  To maintain open communication channels and to listen to feedback
    and comments from the business.

##### Information Security Officer (Head of Security)

1.  To maintain the ISMS.

2.  To carry out internal audits of policies processes and systems
    relating to the ISMS.

3.  To ensure policies, procedures are updated and communicated to those
    who need to know.

4.  To maintain risk, non-conformity, incident, and vulnerability
    registers.

5.  To communicate risks and issues to the ISMS Board that could
    undermine information security.

6.  To communicate good security practices.

7.  To monitor and record progress against outstanding incidents and
    actions, vulnerabilities, risk treatments or security improvements.

##### Executive

1.  To communicate regularly with their team, preferably face to face,
    to ensure information relating to the ISMS is available, understood
    and up to date.

2.  To ensure they and their team are maintaining ISMS records.

3.  To listen to feedback from their team and users and to keep the ISMS
    Board informed.

4.  To communicate the outcomes of any ISMS Risk Assessment or Risk
    Treatment Plan.

##### Community Fibre IT and Security Operations staff

1.  To ensure they are informed and have access to information to be as
    effective as possible in their role.

2.  To ensure they are maintaining good communication practice as set
    out in this document.

3.  To maintain user, project, information and physical and/or virtual
    assets inventories.

4.  To keep line managers, colleagues, and users aware of up-to-date
    information

##### Community Fibre staff

1.  To keep the Information Security Officer informed about their needs
    for data handling.

2.  To address any information security requirements raised with them
    (by i.e., Line manager, Information Security Officer, or the
    Executive team) and to communicate the outcome (for example, by
    completing any Cyber Awareness or GDPR training).

##### Community Fibre suppliers and contractors

1.  To ensure they are maintaining good communication practice as set
    out in this document.

2.  To address any information security requirements raised with them
    and to communicate the outcome.

3.  To keep line managers and Community Fibre internal contacts aware of
    up-to-date information.

##### Communication Channels

1.  The intended audience and their location will determine the most
    suitable channel, and the following factors need to be considered:

2.  Location -- a shared office may restrict what can be communicated. A
    remote user may limit the channels available for use.

3.  Role -- a person's role and relevant expertise may influence whether
    a more suited to interaction and feedback is appropriate.

4.  Impact -- a direct conversation, training sessions or detailed
    documentation may be more suitable than email communication or
    notification for people whose daily work is affected by the issue
    being communicated.

requirements and has implemented the actions described in section 6.1

implement plans to achieve ISMS objectives, as highlighted in sections 3

### Performance evaluation {#performance-evaluation .Heading-3_Green}

##### 9.1 Monitoring, measurement, analysis, and evaluation

Monitoring (see
[6.2](#information-security-objectives-and-planning-to-achieve-them)) is
based on risk and is linked to the risk & opportunities register
together with the risk assessments which are carried out. This is also
monitored through internal audits (section [9.2](#internal-audit)) and
management review (section [9.3](#management-review)) to ensure the
effectiveness of the management system.

##### 9.2 Internal Audit

An internal audit schedule is prepared on an annual basis year and
covers the requirements of the ISO27001 standard. Internal audits are
carried out through "risk or clause based" auditing. Appropriate
personnel are allocated to complete the internal audits and must record
appropriate evidence for completeness. All audits completed must be
authorised by Senior Management as complete once any non-conforming
areas have been dealt with (without any undue delay). Internal audit
documentation must be kept and filed appropriately.

The ISMS management platform
([ISMS.online](https://communityfibre.isms.online/projects/18216/structure))
contains the ISO 27001 Audit Program (three years including GDPR)
including ISMS internal audits 3-year plan.

##### 9.3 Management review

Management reviews take place as a minimum on an annual basis. The
attendees present are "Senior Management" and any other appropriate
persons of the business.

All inputs / outputs are fully documented, and minutes captured in line
with the requirements of the specific ISO standard in which Community
Fibre Limited wish to be certified. Any actions arising from the meeting
must be completed without any undue delay and appropriate evidence filed
with the Management review documentation.

##### 10.1 Continual Improvement

Continual Improvement will be ongoing through various elements of the
Business Management System which is encompassed within this document.

- "ISMS Risks & Treatments" - ISMS management system (ISMS.online) (See
  Clause 5.1, 6.1)

- Internal audits

- Third-party external audits

##### 10.2 Nonconformity and corrective action

Should any non-conformances occur or be identified then an internal
audit report / non-conformance report must be completed to ensure that a
full analysis of the problem is resolved. A summary of all actions will
be maintained within the "ISMS Corrective Actions & Improvements" queue
in Jira. The corrective action plan summary must be completed, as this
then forms part of the Management Review meeting.

Should a nonconformity occur, including those arising from complaints,
internal audits & external third-party assessment Community Fibre
Limited designate the appropriate "Senior Management" representative to
ensure that corrective action including root cause analysis is completed
and implemented to avoid any further occurrences. This is then analysed
and should the risk to the business pose to be "high" then this is then
entered onto the "ISMS Risks & Treatments" section of the ISMS
management system
([ISMS.online](https://communityfibre.isms.online/tools/iso27001_version2013_risk_manager/4914))
(See Clause 6.1) to assist in mitigating the risk to the business.
