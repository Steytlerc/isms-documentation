## Introduction {#introduction .Heading-2_Green_Border}

We recognise the transformative potential of AI tools but also
acknowledge the risks they pose to data privacy and security. This
policy aims to guide employees in using AI responsibly, ensuring
compliance with current regulations including GDPR and UK data
protection laws. It specifically addresses the responsible handling of
sensitive data and proprietary company and customer information. As AI
becomes more prevalent in day-to-day work, this policy ensures alignment
with ethics, laws, and core values.

### Scope {#scope .Heading-3_Green}

This policy applies to all employees, contractors, and third parties who
interact with AI tools within Community Fibre. This includes Community
Fibre provided and publicly available AI tools as well as any company
data used in these tools. Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools have the
potential to automate tasks, improve decision-making, and provide
valuable insights. However, their use also presents challenges related
to information security and data protection. This policy guides
employees on safe and secure AI tool usage, especially when handling
potentially sensitive information.

## Principles and Values {#principles-and-values .Heading-2_Green_Border}

### Introduction {#introduction-1 .Heading-4_Green}

AI Use Principles and Values apply to all Employees on the use or
implementation of AI within Community Fibre, including the use of Large
Language Models (LLM), Generic AI, Generative AI, etc. The design
principles from chapter "4. AI System Onboarding Requirements" also
applies when designing or implementing an AI system.

### Roles & Responsibilities {#roles-responsibilities .Heading-4_Green}

A detailed overview of specific actions, along with the designated
owners for each action, is available in **Section 5.7**, titled
\"**Overview of All Actions with Owners**.\"

### Principles and Values {#principles-and-values-1 .Heading-4_Green}

### Be open and transparent about the use of AI {#be-open-and-transparent-about-the-use-of-ai .Heading-4_Green}

Employees must be open and transparent about the use of AI tools within
their departments and teams. This includes sharing the purpose of the
tool, how it is being utilised, and the potential impact on processes or
decision-making.

### Do not use personal data or confidential information  {#do-not-use-personal-data-or-confidential-information .Heading-4_Green}

It is strictly prohibited to use personal data as defined in the
Community Fibre Data Protection Policy, such as telephone numbers, call
detail records, email-addresses or financial information, for AI
applications without a proper legal ground and security & data
protection measures. Confidential business data with a competitive
advantage must also be carefully protected.

An AI Risk Assessment must be performed in case you need to process
personal data and/or confidential information with an AI tool.

###  Use AI within your Expertise  {#use-ai-within-your-expertise .Heading-4_Green}

Use AI tools only within your own domain of knowledge or expertise. This
means you should understand the output of the AI and be able to verify
it.

###  Guard Against Bias and Discrimination  {#guard-against-bias-and-discrimination .Heading-4_Green}

You must be aware of potential bias in AI systems, especially when these
systems make decisions regarding customer interactions. You should take
care to avoid producing discriminatory results.

### Trust, but Verify  {#trust-but-verify .Heading-4_Green}

Never solely rely on the outcome of AI tools. Always check the results
of AI tools to ensure they remain accurate and relevant.

### Hold yourself accountable for AI use  {#hold-yourself-accountable-for-ai-use .Heading-4_Green}

You are personally responsible for the proper use of AI tools and must
report and rectify any errors or issues. You are responsible for the
outcome as it was produced by yourself.

### Consider implications of AI use  {#consider-implications-of-ai-use .Heading-4_Green}

Always consider whether the use of AI tools is responsible, especially
when it affects customers.

### Be Aware of the AI Tool and its Related Risks  {#be-aware-of-the-ai-tool-and-its-related-risks .Heading-4_Green}

As a user of AI tools, you must be fully aware of the capabilities,
limitations, and potential risks associated with the use of these tools.
This principle emphasises the importance of understanding AI systems to
make informed and responsible decisions.

### Mandatory AI Risk Assessment {#mandatory-ai-risk-assessment .Heading-4_Green}

If you intend to integrate an AI system into Community Fibre network or
process confidential information (see [ISP037-Information Classification
and Handling
Policy](https://communityfibre.sharepoint.com/sites/isms/Shared%20Documents/General/ISMS%20Policy%20Documents/Approved/ISP037-Information%20Classification%20and%20Handling%20Policy.pdf))
or personal data within an AI system, a prior AI Risk Assessment is
mandatory. This assessment is essential to proactively address
compliance, privacy and security risks associated with the AI system\'s
deployment and data handling practices.

### Report issues or concerns  {#report-issues-or-concerns .Heading-4_Green}

If you notice or experience any issues regarding AI system performance
or ethical implications, report this immediately to
<dpo@communityfibre.co.uk>.

### Progressive Implementation of AI Tools {#progressive-implementation-of-ai-tools .Heading-4_Green}

When AI functionalities are progressively integrated into existing
software (e.g., Office 365, Adobe), these also need to be assessed. The
integration should pass through an AI Risk Assessment process to ensure
compliance with Community Fibre policies, even if the software is
Community Fibre IT-provided. This ensures that any new AI capabilities,
such as Microsoft Copilot, do not inadvertently expose personal or
confidential information.

## Design & Implementation of AI tools: Requirements {#design-implementation-of-ai-tools-requirements .Heading-2_Green_Border}

### Introduction  {#introduction-2 .Heading-4_Green}

This chapter outlines the critical design requirements for ensuring the
trustworthiness of AI systems within Community Fibre. It underscores the
need to address a multitude of criteria valued by stakeholders. It
highlights key characteristics, including validity, reliability, safety,
security, accountability, transparency, explainability,
interpretability, privacy enhancement and fairness with bias management.

These requirements must be carefully balanced within the specific
context of AI system deployment to mitigate negative consequences
effectively. \[ref. 21\]

### Roles & Responsibilities {#roles-responsibilities-1 .Heading-4_Green}

A detailed overview of specific actions, along with the designated
owners for each action, is available in Section 5.7, titled \"Overview
of All Actions with Owners.\"

### Design Requirements {#design-requirements .Heading-4_Green}

### Valid and Reliable {#valid-and-reliable .Heading-4_Green}

AI systems must be validated for their specific intended use, ensuring
accuracy and reliability. Ongoing testing and monitoring are required to
confirm systems perform as intended.

### Safe {#safe .Heading-4_Green}

AI systems should be designed, developed, and deployed to avoid
endangering human life, health, property, or the environment.

### Secure and Resilient  {#secure-and-resilient .Heading-4_Green}

AI systems must be secure against unauthorised access and use,
maintaining confidentiality, integrity, and availability. Refer to
Community Fibre ISP001-ISMS Mandatory Clauses for applicable security
policies, standards and guidelines.

Systems should be resilient, able to withstand and recover from adverse
events and changes in their environment.

### Accountable and Transparent  {#accountable-and-transparent .Heading-4_Green}

AI systems must have clear accountability mechanisms, e.g. ensuring that
operations, decisions, and methodologies of AI systems are
understandable and traceable.

Transparency in AI operations, including decision-making processes,
should be maintained, and documented.

### Explainable and Interpretable  {#explainable-and-interpretable .Heading-4_Green}

AI systems should be explainable, with explicit representation of their
operational mechanisms. Generic AI might have some limitations in terms
of providing assurance of privacy and security such as general accuracy,
lack of depth, lack of QA, resource intensive, lack of explainability
(model design and operation may be difficult to explain).

When the use of an AI system has a legally binding effect on a data
subject, or similarly significantly affects them, they must be given
meaningful information about the logic of the AI system involved.
Additional requirements from the Community Fibre Data Protection Policy
apply.

### Privacy-Enhanced (if personal data will be processed)  {#privacy-enhanced-if-personal-data-will-be-processed .Heading-4_Green}

AI systems should adhere to the Community Fibre Data Protection Policy
protecting user autonomy and data. A data privacy impact assessment
should be performed to guide AI system design to enhance privacy,
including using privacy-enhancing technologies (. e.g., anonymisation
and encryption).

### Fair -- with Harmful Bias Managed  {#fair-with-harmful-bias-managed .Heading-4_Green}

AI systems must be designed and operated to manage and mitigate harmful
biases.

The AI system treats everyone equally, no matter their background or
what group they belong to. This means the system should not make
decisions that unfairly favour one group over another or discriminate
based on things like a person\'s race, gender, age, or any other
personal characteristic.

### Compliant Design {#compliant-design .Heading-4_Green}

AI Systems must be frequently reviewed using the assessments referred to
in 4.4, to ensure it stays compliant with applicable laws and
regulations, e.g. the EU AI Act and the DPA 2018/EU GDPR.

## Accountability and Governance {#accountability-and-governance .Heading-2_Green_Border}

### Introduction {#introduction-3 .Heading-4_Green}

When introducing a new AI system at Community Fibre, it\'s essential to
fully understand its role, the risks involved, and its purpose. To
ensure smooth integration, Community Fibre has established specific
onboarding requirements. These requirements are designed to identify and
address the risks and actions associated with each AI tool based on its
unique features and intended use. This approach helps us integrate AI
systems effectively and responsibly, ensuring they align with our
organisational goals and standards.

### Roles & Responsibilities  {#roles-responsibilities-2 .Heading-4_Green}

A detailed overview of specific actions, along with the designated
owners for each action, is available in **Section 5.7,** titled
\"**Overview of All Actions with Owners.**\"

### AI tools in existing systems {#ai-tools-in-existing-systems .Heading-4_Green}

When AI functionalities are progressively integrated into existing
software (e.g., Office 365, Adobe), these also need to be assessed. The
integration should pass through an AI Risk Assessment process to ensure
compliance with Community Fibre policies, even if the software is
Community Fibre IT-provided. This ensures that any new AI capabilities,
such as Microsoft Copilot, do not inadvertently expose personal or
confidential information.

### AI systems for one-off or testing purposes {#ai-systems-for-one-off-or-testing-purposes .Heading-4_Green}

When using AI tools \[ref. 13, 15, 16\] for singular tasks or testing
purposes the data shall remain within a secure and controlled
environment; this shall be enforced contractually and with technical
controls that can be demonstrated, and the AI System Registration and
Intake Process shall be completed as part of project initiation or
before one-off use.

### Integrated AI Functionalities {#integrated-ai-functionalities .Heading-4_Green}

New AI features in existing software shall be evaluated for potential
risks, especially regarding data privacy and security. Employees must be
trained on the use of AI functionalities and made aware of risks to
privacy and security.

### AI System Registration and Intake Process  {#ai-system-registration-and-intake-process .Heading-4_Green}

Every integrated AI system and tool proposed or in use within Community
Fibre shall be registered using the designated AI Intake Form. The
purpose of the Intake Form is:

- To collect essential information about the AI system, including its
  purpose, data usage, and technical specifications.

- To conduct an initial evaluation of potential risks associated with
  the AI system, it can be decided, based on the initial assessment,
  that the AI system poses too high a risk for use in Community Fibre.

- To decide whether a comprehensive Privacy Assessment and/or AI Risk
  Assessment is necessary based on the preliminary findings.

The intake for can be found here: [AI System Intake - CFL IT Support
Portal - Jira Service Management
(atlassian.net)](https://communityfibre.atlassian.net/servicedesk/customer/portal/1/group/2/create/1872)

The information gathered from the AI Intake Form will be used to
maintain an up-to-date AI Register, which serves as a centralised record
of all AI systems and tools used within Community Fibre.

### AI Register Maintenance  {#ai-register-maintenance .Heading-4_Green}

Governance, Risk & Compliance will be responsible for maintaining the AI
Register. This role includes:

- Keeping oversight to ensure that the AI Register accurately reflects
  all AI systems and tools in use and that they are compliant with
  Community Fibre's AI policies and relevant regulations.

- Supporting AI project teams, IT, legal, and compliance departments to
  ensure complete and accurate information is captured in the AI
  Register.

- Managing access to the AI Register, ensuring that relevant
  stakeholders have the necessary visibility while maintaining
  confidentiality and data security.

- Technical Owners are responsible for initiating the AI intake process,
  providing essential information about the AI system, and ensuring the
  information remains current.

### Thresholds for Conducting Assessments  {#thresholds-for-conducting-assessments .Heading-4_Green}

### An AI Risk Assessment should be conducted:  {#an-ai-risk-assessment-should-be-conducted .Heading-4_Green}

For any new AI system or major update to an existing system covering one
or more below:

- If the AI system has the potential to significantly impact
  individuals, business operations, or decision-making processes.

- When the AI system processes or potentially processes confidential
  information or strictly confidential data. ([ISP037-Information
  Classification and Handling
  Policy](https://communityfibre.sharepoint.com/sites/isms/Shared%20Documents/General/ISMS%20Policy%20Documents/Approved/ISP037-Information%20Classification%20and%20Handling%20Policy.pdf))

- For AI systems that are complex or use novel technologies, where risks
  might not be fully understood.

On a regular basis for existing AI systems to reassess risks and account
for changes or updates.

### A Privacy Impact Assessment (DPIA) should be conducted: {#a-privacy-impact-assessment-dpia-should-be-conducted .Heading-4_Green}

- Whenever an AI system collects, processes, stores, or transmits
  personal data.

- When there are significant changes to how personal data is processed,
  which could affect user privacy.

- To ensure compliance with data protection laws (UK DPA 2018/EU GDPR).

- On a regular basis (at every 2 years) for existing AI systems to
  reassess privacy risks and account for changes or updates.

Assessments must be reviewed and updated, if needed, at least biannually
or whenever the AI system undergoes significant changes. These
assessments should be conducted to verify that the implemented measures
remain adequate in relation to its security, privacy, and other relevant
risks, as well as to proactively identify and mitigate evolving security
threats.

### Documentation of controls {#documentation-of-controls .Heading-4_Green}

For every AI system, the following must be documented:

- The outcomes of AI Risk Assessments, Privacy Assessments, and Security
  Impact Assessments.

- All measures and controls implemented to mitigate identified risks,
  protect privacy, and ensure security.

Documentation must be updated:

- Whenever a new assessment is conducted, or an existing assessment is
  updated.

- Following the implementation of any new measures or controls, or
  significant adjustments to existing ones.

- To reflect any changes in AI system design, operation, or use that
  could influence the effectiveness of existing controls.

## Roles and Responsibilities {#roles-and-responsibilities .Heading-2_Green_Border}

### Introduction {#introduction-4 .Heading-4_Green}

This chapter outlines the roles and responsibilities regarding the
design, development, implementation, and use of AI within Community
Fibre. .

An overview of specific actions with specific actions owner can be found
in **5.7 Overview of all actions with owners.**

### Business Owner {#business-owner .Heading-4_Green}

- The respective management responsible for the procurement and
  initiating the implementation or use of an AI system will be deemed
  Business Owner.

- The Business Owner has the following responsibilities:

  - Ensure that objectives for the AI system align with overall business
    goals.

  - Develop a strategic plan for AI implementation, including timelines
    and resource allocation.

  - The Business Owner is responsible for overseeing the overall
    compliance of the AI system with Community Fibre policies, laws,
    regulations, and industry standards, including the AI Policy and the
    Data Protection Policy.

  - This role involves ensuring strategic alignment and accountability,
    providing guidance and support to the Technical Owner, and securing
    the resources necessary for effective implementation and compliance.

  - Establish accountability for decisions made by the AI system.

### Technical Owner  {#technical-owner .Heading-4_Green}

- The Technical Owner responsible for the development, testing and
  deployment and maintenance of AI systems, will be deemed as
  responsible Technical Owner.

- The Technical Owner has the following responsibilities:

  - Develop, test, and deploy AI models aligned with company objectives.

  - Ensure that AI design requirements are considered.

  - Monitor AI systems for performance and ethical compliance.

  - Manage data quality and ethical sourcing for AI models.

  - Adhere to internal AI ethical guidelines and best practices.

  - Identify and mitigate operational risks in AI systems.

  - Report AI performance and risks to management and stakeholders

### Governance, Risk & Compliance (GRC) {#governance-risk-compliance-grc .Heading-4_Green}

- GRC is the responsibility of the ISMS Board, chaired by the Head of
  Security

- The Head of Security will be responsible for:

  - AI Register Maintenance and oversight of AI Intakes

  - Establish policies and guidelines for ethical AI usage.

  - Defining and monitoring of AI Controls.

  - Facilitate risk assessments focusing on ethical, legal, and
    operational aspects of AI.

  - Support compliance with external regulations and internal AI
    policies.

  - Provide guidance on AI risk management and ethical considerations.

  - Facilitate training on AI risk, ethics, and compliance.

  - Monitor AI systems and processes for compliance and effectiveness.

### Community Fibre Technical Architecture Team {#community-fibre-technical-architecture-team .Heading-4_Green}

Community Fibre's Architecture team consists of subject matter experts
and collaborate and obtain functional and non-functional requirements
from the business. The core team should at least be represented by
experts in:

- IT

- Data Science

- Mass market marketing

- Data Protection

- Compliance and ethics

- Legal & Regulatory

Community Fibre's Architecture team has the following responsibilities:

- Provide multidisciplinary insights on AI projects, bridging gaps
  between technical, legal, compliance, and business perspectives.

- Review AI projects for ethical considerations, including data privacy,
  fairness, and transparency.

- Assist in interpreting AI policies in the context of specific
  projects, ensuring compliance and alignment.

- Support a cohesive approach to AI implementation and governance.

### End-Users of AI Systems  {#end-users-of-ai-systems .Heading-4_Green}

End-users of AI Systems have the following responsibilities:

- Provide feedback on AI system usability and effectiveness.

- Adhere to the Community Fibre AI Policy, guidelines and best practices
  for AI system usage.

- Participate in training and awareness programs related to AI.

- Report issues or concerns regarding AI system performance or ethical
  implications.
