### General Information {#general-information .Heading-3_Green}

### Document Objective {#document-objective .Heading-3_Purple}

**This document meets the following clauses:**

- Information Security Management System BS EN ISO/IEC 27001:2022 --
  A.5.19: Information security in supplier relationships

- The Electronic Communications (Security Measures) Regulations 2022
  (the \"Regulations\"), Regulations 3 (Network architecture), 4
  (Protection of data and network functions), and associated
  requirements under the Code of Practice, including sections on supply
  chain (Section 6) and third-party supplier measures.

The purpose of this Policy is state management expectations for how we
manage all Third-Party Service Providers that are permitted to access
our systems or store, process or transmit our information.

Supplier selection, management, and control remains a critical challenge
for our organisation. This policy includes the requirements of ISO
27001:2022 and aligns with the Regulations and Code of Practice to
mitigate security risks in supply chains. Our aim is to adopt sensible
practices such as supplier tiering based on value, risk, and assessed
security risk (as defined in Regulation 2 \[**2**\]).

This policy extends beyond traditional information security to include
network architecture resilience, data protection, supply chain
monitoring, and recovery planning, as mandated by the Regulations. We
must demonstrate evidence of contracts, key contacts (e.g., supplier
Data Protection Officers), due diligence, and controls, particularly for
suppliers handling personal data, sensitive data (as defined in
Regulation 2), or security critical functions.

This Policy is supported by the detailed [ISD005 Third Party Security
Standard](https://communityfibre.sharepoint.com/:b:/r/sites/isms/Shared%20Documents/General/ISMS%20Policy%20Documents/Approved/ISD005-Third%20Party%20Security%20Standard.pdf?csf=1&web=1&e=sGAXKi)
(the \"**Standard**\"), which sets out specific security obligations for
suppliers. As of March 2025, all relevant suppliers must agree to comply
with the Standard as a condition of contract, including signing Appendix
I of the Standard to acknowledge acceptance. Non-compliance with the
Standard constitutes a breach of this Policy and may trigger
remediation, audits, or termination.

ISO 27001:2022 expands supplier controls under A.5.19 to include
monitoring, incident response, and termination, with cross-references to
other controls (e.g., A.5.7 Threat intelligence, A.5.23 Information
security for use of cloud services). This policy also addresses
interested parties under Clause 4.2, including regulators like Ofcom,
and incorporates Code of Practice guidance on third-party supplier
measures to reduce risks of security compromises.

### Background {#background .Heading-3_Green}

Not all suppliers are equal, and a risk-based, tiered approach is
essential to avoid disproportionate costs or elevated risks. Community
Fibre organises supplier relationships into Supplier Relationship Types,
aligning with ISO 27001:2022 A.5.19 and the Code of Practice\'s tiering
system for operators (Tier 1: high-risk networks; Tier 2/3: lower-risk).
Suppliers are segmented based on the information assets they access, the
assessed security risk they pose (per Regulation 11(b)), and their role
in our public electronic communications network/service.

Supplier allocation considers two dimensions:

> \(1\) Risk & Importance (consequences of failure, including security
> compromises as defined in section 105A of the Communications Act
> 2003), and
>
> \(2\) Financial Value (anticipated annual spend net of VAT).

The Regulations require us to identify and reduce risks to network
functions \[**2**\], including those involving suppliers (e.g.,
procurement and configuration of equipment under Regulation 3(3)(e)).
The Code of Practice\'s supply chain section (6) emphasises due
diligence, contractual protections, and monitoring for high-risk
suppliers. All suppliers must adhere to minimum security measures, with
higher tiers requiring evidence of compliance (e.g., ISO 27001
certification or equivalent).

### Supplier Relationship Types {#supplier-relationship-types .Heading-3_Green}

The structure below aligns with ISO 27001:2022 A.5.19 and Code of
Practice recommendations (Sections 2.6 Supply chain and 3 Third party
supplier measures).

#### Infrastructure Critical {#infrastructure-critical .Heading-4_Purple}

High-importance, high-risk suppliers integral to day-to-day services,
including datacentre providers, hosting services, and outsourced
developers. These involve access to infrastructure-critical assets,
sensitive data (Regulation 2), or security-critical functions (e.g.,
network signalling planes). Examples: Data centres, AWS or Azure
hosting, TCS and Cognizant outsourced developers accessing source code
or personal data and Orange Moldova remote network operations. Aligned
with Code of Practice Tier 1 measures for high-risk supply chain
elements. Must sign and comply with the full Standard \[**5**\],
including Appendices A, D, E, F, and G where applicable.

#### Strategic Business Services {#strategic-business-services .Heading-4_Purple}

Lower-spend but high-impact suppliers processing
commercial-in-confidence information or lower-volume personal data.
Examples: SaaS-type solutions, telesales services, services/banks. Focus
on confidentiality/integrity risks, with due diligence under Regulation
3(3)(a) for exposed functions. Must sign and comply with relevant
sections of the Standard \[**5**\], including Appendices A and B where
applicable.

#### Other technology {#other-technology .Heading-4_Purple}

Medium-to-low risk suppliers for tools like bug management software,
devices (e.g., laptops without sensitive data), or repair services. No
routine personal data processing; occasional access to
commercial-in-confidence info. Must acknowledge and comply with core
sections of the Standard \[**5**\].

#### Other business services {#other-business-services .Heading-4_Purple}

Lowest-risk, commodity suppliers (e.g., stationery, hotels) with minimal
interaction. Must follow cyber security practices for payment data
(e.g., PCI DSS compliance). No access to sensitive data or critical
functions.

### Controls and approaches {#controls-and-approaches .Heading-3_Green}

Selection, negotiation, and management follow the supplier contract
lifecycle, risk-based tiering, and Regulations/Code of Practice
requirements. All contracts for relevant suppliers must incorporate the
Standard \[**5**\], requiring explicit agreement to comply and signature
of Appendix I. Enforcement includes audit rights (per Standard section
1.7), mandatory remediation at supplier cost for non-compliance (section
1.9), and notification of changes (section 1.10).

- **Pre-contract**: Risk assessments per A.5.19 and Regulation 11(b),
  including supply chain due diligence (Code of Practice 6) and review
  of supplier\'s ability to meet Standard requirements. Use tools like
  OneTrust Vendor Risk Management for profiling.

- **Planned**: Negotiating; hold info in OneTrust. Include Standard as a
  contractual exhibit; obtain pre-signature review.

- **Live**: Active delivery; monitor per A.5.19 c)-m) and Regulation
  3(3)(f) (UK sovereignty for operations). Annual confirmation of
  Standard compliance.

- **In renewal**: Renegotiate; assess ongoing compliance with Standard
  via audits or attestations.

- **Being decommissioned**: Wind-down; ensure data return/destruction
  per A.5.19 m) and Standard section 5 (Data Security).

- **Ended**: Retain records for audits (minimum 3 years per Regulation
  3(4)), including signed Standard copies.

Contract segmentation is also an important part of the lifecycle process
during selection and is determined by two dimensions, Risk & Importance
along with Financial Value.

### Risk & Importance {#risk-importance .Heading-3_Green}

Consider the consequences of contract failure. It is determined simply
as follows, a bit like the risk assessment impact criteria:

- **Very Low** - negligible or no damage to business operation and
  reputation with no costs or consequences

- **Low** - Low damage to business operation or reputation with
  negligible costs or consequences

- **Medium** - Some damage or loss to business operation or reputation
  with some cost exposure but not affecting prospects

- **High** - Serious but not complete damage to business operation or
  reputation with large cost exposure and affecting prospects

- **Very High** - Severe damage to business operation or reputation with
  very large cost exposure and leaving no prospects

Financial Value simply considers the anticipated annual contract spend
with the supplier net of VAT.

**Note** -- Categorising the contract in this manner not only provides a
useful sanity check for the approach towards supplier management, but it
also helps test the appropriateness of the Supplier Type too.
Pre-contract selection work is therefore based on the Supplier type,
along with the Risk & Importance of the contract and its Financial Value
as outlined below.

### Infrastructure Critical {#infrastructure-critical-1 .Heading-3_Green}

Highest risk/importance; align with Code of Practice
Management/Signalling Planes 1-3- and Third-Party Supplier Measures 1-3.

- Limited negotiation; contract on supplier terms with security riders
  referencing the Standard. Due diligence by Head of Operations,
  production team, CFO/CIO, including supply chain audits (Regulation
  3(3)(a)). Require ISO 27001/equivalent; Privacy Impact Assessments
  (A.5.35 Privacy impact assessment) for GDPR/DPA compliance. Mandatory
  Standard \[**5**\] signature.

<!-- -->

- Outsourced developers and administrators - Treat as staff; vetting,
  NDAs (A.5.23). Document in Link Squares; operational info in issue
  trackers.

- All information that is documented about the relationship, commercial
  terms and strategic activity is held within the relevant supplier
  folder in SharePoint.

- Information held includes the commercial agreement between the
  parties, relevant non-disclosure terms, key contacts including the
  Data Protection Officer and any private internal information discussed
  between the team who interact with the supplier. It may also be linked
  to a PIA/information security project if appropriate as part of a new
  system consideration.

- Operational delivery information shared between the parties is usually
  held within the providers issue management system or our own
  operational work management solutions.

### Strategic Business Services {#strategic-business-services-1 .Heading-3_Green}

Contracts are normally of higher risk & importance.

- Identification by staff/CFO/CIO; specs and assessments (e.g.,
  TOPSCORER methodology).

- Require Standard \[**5**\] signature for data-handling services.

- Due diligence is undertaken to ensure that the supplier is a member of
  their own professional body i.e., Institute of Chartered Accountants,
  Law Society, etc., and that they are authorised by the appropriate
  regulatory authority i.e., Financial Conduct Authority (FCA),
  Prudential Regulation Authority (PRA), etc. The TOPSCORER methodology
  can again be considered (more mentally/light touch) here to aid
  selection decisions for any new strategic business services suppliers.

- These suppliers have their key contact and high-level contract
  information held in a supplier account but other information during
  service delivery is held in emails with the supplier, and private
  collaboration groups that generally involve the stakeholders.

### Other technology {#other-technology-1 .Heading-3_Green}

Contracts are generally medium to lower risk & importance.

- Typically, these suppliers are recognised brands for services, and
  high street / commodity internet-based retailers who have standard
  terms and conditions that we adopt as part of the normal purchasing
  decision. If a new requirement can be easily incorporated with our
  existing technologies, we should use that service. If unclear, then
  defer to the CIO or Head of IT Operations for a decision.

- Otherwise, consideration to prospective suppliers is given in terms of
  the requirements and appropriate research is undertaken to secure the
  service at the lowest total cost. We have a cloud first policy and if
  a software as a service provider (SaaS) is considered we ensure they
  meet our expectations for information security given the nature of the
  information placed on their platform. If a device is purchased e.g., a
  laptop we 'spot buy' from recognised retailers e.g., Amazon, Ebuyer,
  Apple, PC World.

- Not all these suppliers are managed with formal accounts as it is not
  worth it, and most are treated as low-level supplier activity where
  issues such as warranty are addressed by exception following normal
  supplier support channels. Warranty and service information is held as
  part of the financial accounts and expenses processes.

### Other business services {#other-business-services-1 .Heading-3_Green}

Contracts are generally the lowest risk & importance.

- Other business services suppliers such as stationery, hotels and
  day-to-day materials that support the business are generally selected
  based on similar principles to other technology services.

- Where ecommerce is involved the company or personal credit card
  information may also be provided to place an initial order, again in
  compliance with the other ISMS controls. On occasion email orders are
  placed but no credit card information is shared via email and payment
  is made via normal banking channels on receipt of an authorised
  invoice.

### Supplier management controls {#supplier-management-controls .Heading-3_Green}

To align with regulatory and compliance requirements (ISO 27001:2022
A.5.19 and Code of Practice Section 2.6/3), the [ISD005-Third Party
Security
Standard](https://communityfibre.sharepoint.com/sites/isms/Shared%20Documents/General/ISMS%20Policy%20Documents/Approved/ISD005-Third%20Party%20Security%20Standard.pdf)
provides the enforceable baseline for these controls:

a\) **Information security in supplier relationships** (ISO 27001:2022
A.5.19): Includes requirements for applicable Regulations and Standard
compliance.

b\) **Minimum Requirements**: Based on [ISP037-Information
Classification and Handling
Policy](https://communityfibre.sharepoint.com/:b:/r/sites/isms/Shared%20Documents/General/ISMS%20Policy%20Documents/Approved/ISP037-Information%20Classification%20and%20Handling%20Policy.pdf?csf=1&web=1&e=Mic7ke)
(A.5.12 classification); NDAs (A.5.14 Information transfer) for critical
tiers. Suppliers must sign the
[Standard](https://communityfibre.sharepoint.com/sites/isms/Shared%20Documents/General/ISMS%20Policy%20Documents/Approved/ISD005-Third%20Party%20Security%20Standard.pdf)
to confirm adherence.

c\) **Information Access**:

- **Infrastructure Critical**: In alignment with [ISP034-Access Control
  Policy](https://communityfibre.sharepoint.com/:b:/r/sites/isms/Shared%20Documents/General/ISMS%20Policy%20Documents/Approved/ISP034-Access%20Control%20Policy.pdf?csf=1&web=1&e=QLmjEv)
  (A.5.15 Access control) for access to Strictly Confidential/sensitive
  data; security critical functions.

- **Strategic**: Commercial-in-confidence, as above.

- **Others**: Limited; per A.5.15 Access control and Standard section 4.

d\) **Monitoring**: A.5.19 e); includes uptime reports, sprint reviews,
financial updates. Third-party audits (e.g., Ofcom/UKAS) against the
Standard. Assessed security risk reviews annually (Regulation 11(b));
Community Fibre reserves audit rights per Standard section 1.7.

e\) **Accuracy/Completeness**: Delivery confirmations; performance
insights (Code of Practice 5 Monitoring and analysis).

f\) **Obligations**: NDAs/supplier agreements for confidentiality; ISO
27001 for large providers. Full Standard compliance enforced
contractually, with remediation at supplier expense for breaches
(Standard section 1.9).

g\) **Incident Handling**: Per agreements; escalate to A.5.24 Security
incident management procedure. Supplier incidents trigger
non-conformities (A.5.25 Assessment and treatment of information
security events) and Standard section 8 reporting.

h\) **Resilience/Recovery**: Integrated into A.5.29 Information security
during disruption; supplier BCP alignment per Standard section 9.

i\) **Awareness/Training**: Per A.5.41 Security awareness and training;
procurement follows A.5.19 j). Use PIAs for GDPR.

j\) **Documentation**: Signed agreements for critical access (A.5.19
l)), including Standard Appendix I.

k\) **Changes/Transitions**: A.5.19 m); includes patching/updates
(A.5.20 Addressing information security within supplier agreements) and
testing (Code of Practice 13). Suppliers must notify changes per
Standard section 1.10.

All staff/suppliers must report risks; oversight by Head of Security,
CIO and Head of Network Technology. Non-compliance with the Standard may
result in contract termination.
